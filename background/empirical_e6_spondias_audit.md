# E6 empirical audit — *Spondias purpurea* joint interaction–connectivity–genetic fragmentation state

## Status

This is a quantitative audit of Cristóbal-Pérez et al. (2021), *Biological Conservation* 256:109007, DOI `10.1016/j.biocon.2021.109007`.

The study is unusually important for the present condition map because it measured, within the same tropical dry-forest fragmentation comparison, multiple layers that are usually split across separate studies:

- pollinator assemblage and visitation (`I`);
- male and female reproductive output (`F`);
- realized pollen flow by paternity assignment (`C_pollen`);
- correlated paternity / effective sire number (`G_mating`);
- adult, seed and juvenile genetic diversity and inbreeding (`G` by cohort);
- continuous versus fragmented habitat context (`M/H`).

It therefore approximates a **synchronized natural joint-state test** much more closely than a habitat-label comparison.

## Fragmentation reduced realised interaction support without replacing the pollinator guild

Stingless bees were the principal pollinators, followed by wasps and flies. The pollinator assemblage did not differ strongly between continuous and fragmented forest, but visitation rate was higher in continuous habitat.

That distinction is central to the current framework: **partner presence/composition and realised interaction support are not interchangeable**. The same broad pollinator guild can remain present while interaction frequency falls.

Natural chain:

`fragmented forest -> realised visitation I ↓`

rather than simply

`fragmented forest -> pollinator richness ↓`.

## Female function declined even though flower production did not

Trees in fragmented habitat produced more flowers overall, yet fruit set was higher in continuous habitat.

This is another direct coarse-state failure. A larger floral display or greater raw resource production did not imply maintained realised reproductive function.

For the empirical state map, the relevant endpoint is therefore **successful reproduction**, not flower number alone.

## Contemporary pollen connectivity contracted strongly

Paternity assignment resolved 312 offspring with >80% confidence to pollen donors within the analysed populations.

Realised pollen-flow distance differed sharply between habitat conditions:

- continuous forest: mean **209.15 ± 19.28 m**;
- fragmented forest: mean **44.91 ± 1.98 m**.

Observed ranges were:

- continuous: **7.7–828.2 m**;
- fragmented: **2.98–134 m**.

More than 75% of assigned pollination events in continuous habitat occurred over >75 m, whereas only about 32% did so in fragmented habitat.

Thus the relevant natural connectivity condition is not a generic `migration` scalar. It is an observed contraction of the **effective pollen-dispersal kernel**.

## Fragmentation reduced sire diversity

Mean multilocus correlated paternity (`r_p`) was approximately:

- continuous habitat: **0.55**, corresponding to effective sire number `N_ep ≈ 2.58`;
- fragmented habitat: **0.63**, corresponding to `N_ep ≈ 1.58`.

The habitat contrast therefore points in the same direction as the pollen-distance result: offspring in fragments draw from a narrower realized paternal pool.

The exact site values were heterogeneous, so this is not a universal paternity threshold. The biological condition is **reduced realized donor diversity**.

## Genetic consequences are cohort-specific

Adult genetic diversity and spatial genetic structure were broadly comparable between continuous and fragmented conditions, but the negative genetic signal was stronger in younger cohorts.

Published summary values show, for example:

- continuous juveniles observed heterozygosity `H_o ≈ 0.465` and fixation index `F ≈ 0.06`;
- fragmented juveniles `H_o ≈ 0.272` and `F ≈ 0.461`;
- continuous seeds `H_o ≈ 0.462`, `F ≈ 0.059`;
- fragmented seeds `H_o ≈ 0.311`, `F ≈ 0.379`.

Adults in fragments still retained substantial allelic diversity even while contemporary mating and offspring cohorts already showed degradation.

This is a second natural example, alongside *Conospermum undulatum*, of why **cohort identity belongs in the state**. Standing adult neutral diversity can lag contemporary interaction and mating deterioration.

## Natural condition recovered

The strongest natural condition is:

> **joint interaction–connectivity limitation with cohort-emergent genetic deterioration.**

Operationally:

`I_realised ↓ + C_pollen kernel contracts + donor diversity ↓ + F_reproduction ↓`

followed by

`G_seed/juvenile deteriorates before adult G necessarily shows the same magnitude of change`.

This is closer to the complete manuscript mechanism than any single urban/island label because it connects the ecological interaction, direct function, contemporary movement and genetic consequence in one natural fragmentation comparison.

## Why this matters for genetic warning

The current model shows that genetic warning can be reproducible inside a fixed loss-generating domain but is not automatically portable across different eco-genetic domains. *Spondias* gives the natural measurement analogue of the same ordering problem.

If only adult heterozygosity were monitored, the system could appear genetically buffered while current visitation, pollen movement, donor diversity and offspring genetics have already shifted. Monitoring should therefore align:

1. current interaction support;
2. current process-specific connectivity;
3. direct function;
4. offspring/juvenile genetic state;
5. adult historical genetic state.

A warning metric cannot be interpreted without specifying which cohort and process it represents.

## Prospective state-sufficiency test

A decisive reanalysis or new temporal resurvey should compare future reproductive/recruitment function after conditioning on:

- local adult/flowering density (`D`);
- visitation/effectiveness (`I`);
- sex ratio and mate availability (`R/T` where appropriate);
- paternity-derived pollen-dispersal kernel and sire diversity (`C/G_mating`);
- adult and offspring cohort genetics (`G_adult`, `G_offspring`);
- fragment identity, matrix and fragmentation history (`M`);
- joint spatial alignment among high interaction support, compatible mates, pollen connectivity and genetic state (`A`).

Then add habitat category/history last. If `fragmented/continuous` still improves held-out prediction, the measured state is incomplete; the category itself should not be promoted to a mechanistic regime.

## Cross-system interpretation

The four strongest natural anchors now span distinct mechanisms:

- *Crepis sancta*: local interaction limitation despite nonzero wider movement;
- Miyake *Camellia–Zosterops*: movement-mediated compensation despite low local resources;
- *Conospermum undulatum*: contemporary pollen-flow/reproductive deterioration with adult-genetic historical lag;
- *Spondias purpurea*: synchronized interaction, pollen-flow, reproductive and cohort-genetic deterioration.

Together they show that the same coarse descriptor `fragmented` can map to loss, compensation or lag depending on the measured joint state.
