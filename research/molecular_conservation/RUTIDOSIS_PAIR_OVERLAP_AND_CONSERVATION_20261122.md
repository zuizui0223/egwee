# Rutidosis published-population-pair overlap and conservation endpoint separation (2026-11-22)

**Status: verified published article design/metadata comparison, not a new original-data analysis.** Exact cross-paper list establishes repeated *population-pair identities*, NOT necessarily the same plants, families or seed cohorts. Does not upgrade the EGWE finite mathematical predictions to natural validation.

## Core result: 2012 vs 2013 sampling frame is identical by published pair identifiers

| Original source | Programme and outcome | Published source |
|---|---|---|
| Pickup et al. (2012), *Evolutionary Applications* DOI 10.1111/j.1752-4571.2012.00284.x | 12 local–foreign population pairs; climate/soil reciprocal-origin experimental setting, neutral F_ST vs quantitative Q_ST, germination, seedling survival, adult biomass and number of inflorescences; original Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.1qp8v | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3552408/ |
| Pickup et al. (2013), *Proceedings B* DOI 10.1098/rspb.2012.2058 | 2,455 experimental crosses across 12 population pairs, F1/F2/F3 hybrid/backcross fitness, source population size/diversity/inbreeding | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574427/ |

Both papers explicitly list the **same 12 pair codes in the same order**:

`LW-QB; SR-CC; MA-BA; HH-MA; QB-RH; CR-LW; RH-CF; MJ-GB; GB-PO; SR-TR; CF-SA; GB-SA`.

Therefore **12/12 pair identities overlap**. Both belong to a *shared Rutidosis population-pair frame*. Whether seed families / plants overlap requires original experimental IDs; assume no independent study-level replication for a pooled G×F transport claim without that check.

Other Rutidosis studies: Pickup & Young (2008) uses 24 diploid and five tetraploid source–target pairs; 2010 Young & Pickup observational seven populations. Their pair/population IDs are not fully cross-tabulated in the currently inspected primary material: overlap with the 12-pair frame is **not quantified**. Do not assume independence or exact duplication.

## Published empirical effects that MUST remain author-attributed

1. Pickup & Young (2008): across 1,756 controlled reciprocal crosses in 29 pairs, between-population pollinations improved fertilisation in some pairs by up to 30% versus within-population. This is immediate reproductive function from experimental pollen provenance, not proof of permanent S-allele introduction or next-generation population recovery. Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/6801070 .
2. Pickup et al. (2012): across 12 pairs, average local seedling survival advantage **1.3 ± 0.4 percentage points** (as reported); nonlocal origin had average **16.1% more inflorescences**. Response direction is **endpoint dependent**; this does not show an intervention's long-term establishment or compatible mating function. Neutral F_ST was poorly predictive of local adaptation in this design. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3552408/ .
3. Pickup et al. (2013): in 2,455 crosses across the same 12 pair codes, F1–F3 hybrid/backcross fitness showed no detected outbreeding depression in measured components, and heterosis was more marked for large genetically diverse sources introduced to small inbred recipients. These are published fitness results, not direct field pollination/recruitment rescue or independent replication. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574427/ .
4. Young & Pickup (2010): seven populations relate diallel-estimated mate/S availability, pollen deposition and seed set. Original paper already establishes compatible-mate limitation despite retained pollen delivery; no claim of newly discovered mechanism.

## The mathematical-to-molecular ecological question that remains open

- Functional mating state `G_compatibility` = availability of compatible pollen-donor genotypes, not neutral diversity proxy `H_E` / F_ST.
- Physical and ecological interaction `I` = effective compatible pollen deposition at the recipient, not visits or total pollen load alone.
- Short-term function `F_seed` = compatible fertilisation and seed formation; longer-term `F_recruitment` = viable offspring establishment. F1–F3 offspring fitness W may be supportive yet cannot replace both.
- The exact EGWE patchwise I×G joint-state model needs **within-patch co-observations**; 2008 crosses, 2012 common-garden phenotype, and 2013 F1–F3 crosses **cannot simply be joined by site name to manufacture a joint longitudinal mediation chain**.

## Interventions this source literature can motivate (not validated efficacy prescriptions)

If total deposited pollen is low: increase effective pollinator access. If pollen is abundant but compatible donors are scarce: carefully selected compatible donor pollen / genetic sources. If early seeds increase but later establishment fails: investigate habitat, inbreeding/outbreeding tradeoffs and recruitment. Verify ploidy, local adaptation, biosafety, donor population viability and recipient provenance before translocation.
