## B. Direct, published biological evidence for ecological–genetic decoupling

**Young & Pickup (2010), Journal of Applied Ecology, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2664.2010.01798.x (SEVEN natural populations)**

- Diallel crosses used to **infer** S-allele number and compatible-mate probability. Not direct S-locus sequencing.
- Mean field seed set ranged from **11.8 seeds per inflorescence** in a population of 63 reproductive plants to **39.1** in a population of approximately 95,200. Population size versus mean seed set: published R²=0.67, P=0.016.
- Pollen grains deposited per stigma: **six of seven population means 12.4–14.3**, the exception with size 63 averaged **3.2**; pollen loads did not track log population size overall (P=0.604).
- Correlated paternity: **0.96** at population size 5 versus **0.11** at 95,200 (reported extreme populations), indicating fewer effective pollen fathers in the tiny population. This is not a direct neutral genomic heterozygosity change.
- These are published within-system correlational observations and authors' original interpretation: reduced compatible mating despite broadly retained pollen service. Because population size, compatible mates and other differences covary, do not claim that paper isolated a universal causal mediation coefficient or all small populations had adequate pollen.

**Pickup & Young (2008), Heredity, DOI 10.1038/sj.hdy.6801070 (ONE controlled-cross programme)**
- 1,756 reciprocal experimental crosses across 24 diploid and 5 tetraploid source–target pairs.
- Between-population crossing improved immediate fertilization in some pair comparisons by up to ~30% relative to within-population crosses; larger benefit in small recipient populations (original paper).
- Experimental manipulation supports donor-source relevance to immediate fertilization; not demonstrated permanent genetic introgression, fully realized field pollinator restoration or long-run recruitment recovery.

**Pickup et al. (2012), Evolutionary Applications, DOI 10.1111/j.1752-4571.2012.00284.x (12-pair common-environment origin study)**
- 12 local–nonlocal pairs, original Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.1qp8v.
- Local origin seedling survival advantage 1.3±0.4 percent as reported across pairs, but foreign origin ~16.1% greater mean number of inflorescences, response direction varies by fitness component.
- F_ST, Q_ST and environment provide partly different information; this is NOT a simultaneous S-compatibility × wild effective pollen × direct viable offspring study.

**Pickup et al. (2013), Proceedings B, DOI 10.1098/rspb.2012.2058**
- 2,455 crosses, 12 population pairs across F1–F3 fitness components. Published absence of detected outbreeding depression in measured components and source-dependent heterosis.
- All 12 pair IDs exactly match 2012 paper; shared biological sampling frame, not independent replication and not necessarily identical individual experimental cohorts.

## C. Conservation inference that is and is not warranted

Grounded mechanistic distinction for this taxon:
1. `I_quantity`: pollen arrival can be broadly retained in multiple fragmented populations.
2. `G_mate`: the S-locus mating compatibility pool can be a direct limiting process even with pollen present.
3. `F_seed`: fertilization and seed output depend on *usable* pollen, not merely deposited grains.
4. `W_next`: multi-generation offspring fitness, local adaptation and source provenance require separate testing; seed counts do not guarantee recruits.

Action hypothesis, **not a tested management rule in this programme**: when pollen is scarce, prioritize ecological pollination; when pollen is plentiful but compatible mates scarce, test verified ploidy-compatible donor pollen/seed source; when early fertilization improves but establishment fails, address habitat/recruitment and possible maladaptation. Avoid assuming any SNP heterozygosity threshold diagnoses the binding bottleneck.

**Model transfer remains unverified**: EGWE's patchwise cross-layer covariance and strongest-refuge predictor have not been tested on matched natural site×year×maternal genotypes with effective pollen and future recruits. Existing publications demonstrate a biological example of layer discordance, not the model's exact operator, universal thresholds, or urban/island convergence.
