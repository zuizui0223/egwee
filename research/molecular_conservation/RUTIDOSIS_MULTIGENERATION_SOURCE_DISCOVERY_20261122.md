## Newly located original-source candidates
1. **Pickup et al. (2012), Predicting local adaptation in fragmented plant populations: implications for restoration genetics**. Original article openly states data archived at Dryad DOI **10.5061/dryad.1qp8v**. This is an actual archive locator, not yet a verified file inventory. The article includes environmental, F_ST/Q_ST and trait/fitness comparisons; population-pair dependencies and overlap with earlier *Rutidosis* crossing programmes must be audited before any genetic-function synthesis. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3552408/
2. **Pickup, Field, Rowell & Young (2013), Source population characteristics affect heterosis following genetic rescue of fragmented plant populations.** Published F1/F2/F3 fitness from **2,455 crosses across 12 population pairs**; published results do not establish independent pollinator-mediated ecological-function recovery. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574427/ . This is especially valuable for *persistence of offspring fitness beyond the immediate crossing response*, but should be classified W_fitness, not F_direct unless source endpoints substantiate that distinction.
3. **Rodger et al., Evolutionary history and genetic connectivity across highly fragmented populations of an endangered daisy**. Public DArT-seq raw-read dataset DOI **10.26180/8172842.v3** linked through Monash/Research Data Australia. A molecular resource for ancestry/population structure, not automatically matched to 2008/2010 maternal crossing cohorts. The repository record mentions a download requiring a published access procedure; do not automate credential entry or infer raw-sequence equivalence to S-locus genotyping. https://researchdata.edu.au/the-link-raw-button-wrinklewort/1944677 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8102499/

## Revised identification strategy
- Separate **S-locus functional compatibility** (diallel crossing, no sequence assumption), **genome-wide ancestry/structure** (DArT-seq, source population matching only), and **multigeneration fitness W** (F1–F3 common-garden crossing).
- A population name matching across studies is insufficient to join individual maternal offspring records. Record provenance, generation, population ID, cross, ploidy and independently source-declared keys.
- Do not add the 2012/2013 studies as independent ecological systems: shared *Rutidosis* population histories and experimental programme overlap are likely. Do not count crosses as independent populations.
- **Near-term first action:** inspect official Dryad 10.5061/dryad.1qp8v metadata and original file list, then sheet/header-only source audit; stop on 401. No re-estimation until source keys and outcomes are frozen.
- **Falsifier:** if 2012/2013 only support genomic ancestry and general fitness but not contemporary compatible pollen and viable recruitment in the same mother/cohort, the proposed G×I×F joint natural test remains NOT_IDENTIFIABLE.

## Publication ceiling
The biological point that low S-allele diversity can constrain seed production despite pollen receipt was published by Young & Pickup 2010. The multigeneration outcrossing fitness pattern was also published. New contribution requires independently held-out transfer, a truly matched molecular-to-function causal contrast, or cross-system prediction—not rebranding these existing results.
