## Candidates advanced

### IFQ020 — Kolb 2008, Phyteuma spicatum

The common-source experiment deliberately manipulates two fragmentation components in a factorial design:

- population size: large vs small;
- isolation: non-isolated vs isolated.

Pollinator visitation and reproductive success are measured on the same experimental population units. This advances because the exposure is manipulated prospectively and is not inferred from the response.

Before quantitative admission, the full experimental-cell replication and between-population dispersion must be recovered. Population-size and isolation contrasts must be declared source-faithfully; the analysis may not choose whichever factor gives a larger layer difference.

### IFQ022 — Lopes & Buzato 2007, Psychotria suterella

The source explicitly evaluates three fragmentation categories:

- non-fragmented habitat;
- fragments connected by corridors;
- isolated fragments.

Pollinator assemblage/pollination and reproductive output are measured within the same ten-site landscape network. This is a genuine same-system I-F fragmentation design.

Before effect calculation, site allocation among the three categories and the contrast/dependence rule must be fixed. The connected and isolated fragment categories may not be collapsed or selected after seeing their outcome magnitudes.

## Direct-family closures

- **IFQ017 Trichodiadema strumosum:** one small fragment + one medium fragment + one mainland reference; only one independent reference site.
- **IFQ018 Phlox pilosa:** 27-population local abundance/population-size analysis, not a direct fragmentation contrast.
- **IFQ019 Satyrium longicauda:** 36-population size/density/isolation gradients.
- **IFQ021 Kunin 1997 Brassica/Sinapis:** experimental local population size and density arrays, not habitat-fragmentation landscapes.
- **IFQ023 Tristerix corymbosus:** edge-distance gradient among plants inside one forest fragment.
- **IFQ024 Gentiana pneumonanthe:** 18-population demographic/fragmentation continuum and threshold analysis.

All six remain in the broader systematic/gradient/mechanistic evidence universe.
