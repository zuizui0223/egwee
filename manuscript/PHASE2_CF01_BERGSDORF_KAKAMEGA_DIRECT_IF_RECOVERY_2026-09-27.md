# CFTQ0349 Bergsdorf Kakamega retrospective direct I-F recovery — 2026-09-27

## Admission

`P2_CF01_BERGSDORF_KAKAMEGA_2006` is admitted as **one retrospective direct I-F programme** with four dependent species×campaign panels.

The source site tables and qualitative outcomes were visible before the deterministic recovery rule was written. This is therefore retrospective generalisation / pair-coverage evidence, not outcome-blind confirmation.

## Effect unit

The source study sites are the observational units.

The dissertation explicitly separates named sites inside the Kakamega main forest block from named surrounding forest-fragment sites. The main-forest sites are localities inside one broad continuous block rather than separate continuous-forest landscapes, so the programme is interpreted as a **site/local-context observational fragmentation contrast**.

Flowers, standardized observation units, individual plants, stigmas, fruits and seeds are nested below site and are not fragmentation replicates.

## Deterministic recovered panels

Every species×campaign with both a complete site-level standardized visitation-occurrence table and a site-level mean fruit-set table was retained:

1. *Acanthopale pubescens* — 2001;
2. *Acanthus eminens* — 2002;
3. *Acanthus eminens* — 2003;
4. *Heinsenia diervilleoides* — 2002–2003.

*Dracaena fragrans* remains explicitly blocked because a common site-level I/F table is not recoverable from the indexed public source surface. It is not treated as zero and is not excluded because of effect direction.

## Locked endpoints

Interaction support:

`I = 1 - (standardized observation units with zero source-accepted pollinator visits / all observation units)`.

The source-defined accepted pollinator set is:

- *A. pubescens*: honey bees;
- *A. eminens*: *Xylocopa* carpenter bees;
- *H. diervilleoides*: all visitor groups.

Reproductive function:

- F = source table mean natural fruit set at the same site.

## Primary panel effects

Effects are Hedges g for fragment-site minus main-forest-site values.

### Acanthopale pubescens — 2001

- I: **g = +0.349**
- F: **g = −2.848**
- residual rho(I,F): **+0.437**
- I − F: **+3.197**
- 95% CI: **[+1.208, +5.186]**
- p = **0.00163**

This panel shows strong interaction–function separation: standardized pollinator occurrence is slightly higher in fragment sites while fruit set is substantially lower.

## Programme-level internal gate

The four panels are dependent outcomes inside one programme.

Bonferroni protection across the four recovered panels gives:

- minimum panel p = **0.00163385**
- `p_programme = min(1, 4 × min(p_panel)) = 0.00653539`.

Thus at least one recovered species×campaign resolves cross-layer separation even after protecting against selecting the strongest of the four visible panels.

This does **not** imply a universal I>F ordering across Kakamega species. Three panels have wide I-F intervals crossing zero, and the sign/magnitude of both marginal responses varies among species and years.

## Ecological interpretation

The programme provides a concrete natural example of response-geometry heterogeneity under the same regional fragmentation setting.

Most panels show visitation occurrence maintained or elevated in fragment sites. Reproductive function does not follow a single corresponding rule:

- *Acanthopale* shows maintained/elevated visitation occurrence but sharply lower fruit set;
- *Acanthus* and *Heinsenia* show positive fragment-site responses in both I and F, with imprecise within-panel separation.

This supports a mechanistic interpretation in which the presence or frequency of pollinator visits is not by itself a sufficient state variable for realised reproductive function. Pollen quality, mating structure, heterospecific pollen, resource limitation or other post-visitation filters can alter the I→F mapping, but those mechanisms are not identified causally by the present contrasts.

## Registry consequence

This recovery:

- adds **one** pair-specific direct I-F programme;
- changes direct I-F coverage from **2/5 to 3/5**;
- leaves direct C-F coverage at **2/5**;
- leaves the frozen Phase-1 five-cluster / 17-effect Fisher synthesis unchanged;
- does not add a sixth Phase-1 cluster;
- does not validate an EGWE/NEE finite operator.

## Claim ceiling

The admissible claim is that Kakamega provides one retrospective, covariance-aware, multi-panel direct I-F programme in which fragmentation responses are species/year dependent and at least one panel resolves strong interaction–function separation.

Do not claim that all Kakamega species show reproductive collapse, that fragment sites are independent forest-block experiments, or that pollinator visitation causally mediates the observed F responses.
