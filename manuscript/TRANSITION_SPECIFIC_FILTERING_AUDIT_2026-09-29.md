# Transition-specific filtering topology — exploratory audit

## Status

Post hoc / hypothesis-generating. This audit is not a replacement confirmatory analysis.

## Question

Does habitat fragmentation propagate monotonically through plant reproductive life cycles, or do different biological transitions systematically amplify, attenuate or delay the response?

## Pattern 1 — interaction quantity → reproductive function

Three independent programmes retain a resolved downstream function-dominant geometry under their audited representations:

- *Eucalyptus wandoo* — interaction/pollen quantity increases while seed production declines;
- *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* — pollinator abundance changes weakly while fruit set declines strongly;
- Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens* — visitation occurrence is maintained/slightly elevated while fruit set declines; the geometry survives direct g→lnRR re-expression.

Thus the strongest cross-system process mismatch is **amplification or inversion between measured interaction quantity and realised reproductive function**.

## Pattern 2 — movement/mating support → reproductive function

The three direct movement/mating–F anchors show the opposite point ordering on both Hedges g and lnRR:

- *Serapias*: pollen immigration more negative than fruit set;
- *Brosimum*: correlated-paternity support more negative than progeny vigour;
- *Eucalyptus socialis*: mating support more negative than family growth.

The statistical resolution of these contrasts is scale-sensitive, so this is not a confirmatory 3/3 result. But the **point ordering itself is cross-scale stable in all three direct programmes**.

This suggests attenuation / compensation from movement or mating disruption to immediate downstream performance rather than simple one-to-one transmission.

## Pattern 3 — adult standing genetics → new cohorts

*Spondias* provides a strong within-programme anchor in which juvenile and seed H_O responses are more negative than adult H_O on both g and lnRR.

However the preregistered five-programme adult–offspring synthesis gives mean adult-minus-offspring contrast +0.129 with mKH 95% CI [-0.655, +0.912], p=0.672. Across programmes the direction is mixed.

Therefore **cohort lag is plausible in individual systems but not general across the current replicated family**.

## Ecological synthesis

The current evidence argues against a monotonic life-cycle rule.

> **Fragmentation effects are transition-filtered: interaction quantity can understate downstream reproductive loss, movement/mating disruption can be attenuated before immediate performance, and genetic cohort lag is system-dependent rather than universal.**

This is a more general ecological hypothesis than “one bottleneck layer”. It predicts that each biological transition has its own transfer function.

## Why this is potentially novel

Existing fragmentation meta-analyses largely estimate endpoint-specific average effects or correlations among endpoint classes. Demographic compensation literature asks how vital rates covary across environments. The present lead instead concerns **within-system propagation across reproductive transitions under a common fragmentation exposure**.

## Fresh test

A prospective programme should measure on the same independent populations:

`interaction quantity → effective mating quality → reproductive output → offspring performance / cohort genetics`

and estimate transition-specific gains on a prospectively declared effect scale.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- transition-specific amplification, attenuation and lag occur in the audited systems;
- three independent programmes support a robust interaction-quantity proxy-failure topology;
- three direct movement/mating–F programmes have the same cross-scale point ordering but scale-sensitive significance;
- five-programme adult–offspring evidence rejects a simple universal directional lag.

Not allowed:

- a universal monotonic upstream→downstream gradient;
- a confirmed general compensation mechanism;
- treating the three movement/mating point orderings as a preregistered sign test;
- inferring global transition-gain parameters from the current heterogeneous endpoints.
