## Most informative closure

### CFTQ0257 — Dianella revoluta isolation experiment

Duncan et al. use replicated roadside flower arrays to manipulate:

- distance from a conspecific pollen source;
- near-versus-far proximity to a large nature reserve;
- local conspecific flower density/isolation.

The experiment directly measures outcross and heterospecific pollen receipt and therefore gives
strong interaction / pollen-movement process evidence.

However, the same experimental arrays do **not** measure realised fruit set or seed production.
The paper argues that reduced outcross pollen receipt can lower seed set or increase inbreeding
depending on mating system, but that downstream F response is inferred rather than measured in the
registered fragmentation experiment.

Decision: retain as an I/C mate-isolation mechanism anchor; direct I-F/C-F increment = **0**.

This is an important layer-definition firewall: plausible downstream reproductive consequences are
not promoted to `F_reproductive_function` unless they were observed on the same effect frame.

## Other notable closures

### CFTQ0254 — Gentianopsis ciliata

The source surveys 63 populations and reports reduced seed production in small populations, but the
empirical exposure is **population size**. Pollination limitation is proposed as a mechanism rather
than directly measured over the population frame.

Decision: small-population fecundity evidence only; population size is not recoded as habitat
fragmentation severity.

### CFTQ0255 — Ashworth et al. fragmentation susceptibility paper

This is a conceptual/review explanation of why specialist and generalist plants may show similar
fragmentation susceptibility. It synthesizes earlier studies rather than adding a new primary
programme.

Decision: non-primary.
