# Hidden effective-mating layer hypothesis — 2026-10-03

## Status

Mechanistic triangulation / hypothesis-generating context. The external sources below are **not added to the quantitative denominator** and do not alter the frozen search or effect-size synthesis.

## Core observation from EGWEE

The strongest scale-stable matched result is interaction–function proxy failure:

- *Eucalyptus wandoo*: pollen tubes increase while seed production declines;
- *Cardiopetalum calophyllum*: pollinator abundance changes weakly while fruit set declines strongly;
- Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens*: visitation is maintained/slightly higher while fruit set declines; the mismatch survives g→lnRR re-expression.

At the same time, the complete quantitative I–F set contains **8/8 quantity-level interaction endpoints and 0/8 direct effective-mating-quality endpoints**.

## Mechanistic triangulation from external literature

Independent studies show that fragmentation can alter the identity and compatibility of pollen transfer without an equivalent decline in interaction quantity.

### *Dianella revoluta* — isolation changes outcross pollen, not visitation

Duncan et al. (2004; doi:10.1111/j.1365-2745.2004.00933.x) showed that outcross conspecific pollen receipt declined strongly with isolation, whereas heterospecific pollen deposition—and thus inferred visitation—did not decline. Their conclusion was explicit: reduced plant density and isolation can reduce outcrossing even when pollinator abundance and behaviour are unaffected.

### *Linnaea borealis* — high pollination, low fruiting because compatible mates are missing

Scobie & Wilcock (2009; doi:10.1093/aob/mcp007) found high natural pollination levels but very low fruit set in fragmented patches. Crosses showed that the problem was lack of compatible mates and restricted pollen movement, not simply insufficient pollen arrival.

### *Heliconia tortuosa* — landscape change alters pollinator identity and mating quality

Torres-Vanegas et al. (2021; doi:10.1111/1365-2745.13594) showed that deforestation filtered high-mobility hummingbirds from pollinator communities. The resulting shift reduced pollen-pool haplotype diversity and increased biparental inbreeding. This provides a direct mechanism by which partner identity and movement can degrade mating quality even when total interaction quantity is not the primary pathway.

### *Conospermum undulatum* — quality limitation intensifies in small fragments

Delnevo et al. (2020; doi:10.1016/j.biocon.2020.108824) separated pollen quantity and quality limitation across a fragmentation gradient and found that genetic factors limiting pollen quality were especially important in small fragments.

## General ecological hypothesis

The combined evidence suggests a specific missing conversion layer:

`interaction quantity  →  effective mating quality  →  reproductive function`

Fragmentation can alter:

- who the pollinator is;
- how far it moves;
- which pollen donor is represented;
- whether pollen is compatible, unrelated or outcross;
- whether apparently successful pollen transfer ultimately produces viable seeds.

Therefore the same interaction quantity can translate into different reproductive outcomes.

## Why this is more than 'visitation is noisy'

Classic methods work already shows that visitation is an imperfect proxy for pollinator effectiveness. The stronger hypothesis here is that **fragmentation specifically changes the identity/compatibility content of biological transfer**, so quantity can remain stable even while the functional quality of that transfer degrades.

This interpretation also explains why Wandoo remains informative despite using pollen tubes rather than visitor counts: a quantity metric that is biologically close to fertilization can still fail if it does not encode self/outcross identity, donor diversity or later compatibility filtering.

## Hidden-peak hypothesis

The exploratory topology suggests a more specific prospective prediction:

> **Effective mating quality may be the most fragmentation-sensitive stage in some systems: interaction quantity can understate damage, while final reproductive output can be partly buffered relative to mating disruption.**

This is **not established by the current corpus**, because Q, E and F are not measured synchronously in the same independent programmes. It is a triangulated hypothesis for fresh data.

## Fresh test

On the same fragmented and reference populations, measure:

1. `Q`: visitation / pollen quantity;
2. `E`: compatible pollen, donor diversity, realised paternity, outcrossing or another direct mating-quality metric;
3. `F`: fruit/seed/viable-seed production.

Primary fresh direct estimand remains prospectively scale-aware:

- lnRR for positive Q/E/F quantities;
- Hedges g as mandatory sensitivity;
- no outcome-selected effect scale.

The hidden-peak hypothesis predicts `E` will often deteriorate more than `Q`; the sign and magnitude of `E−F` then distinguish direct propagation from downstream buffering.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- external literature provides independent mechanisms by which fragmentation can decouple interaction quantity from mating quality and reproduction;
- the missing effective-mating layer aligns mechanistically with the current 8/8 quantity-only measurement gap;
- Wandoo demonstrates that proxy failure can persist even at the pollen-tube stage;
- Q→E→F is a biologically grounded prospective design.

Not allowed:

- effective mating is universally the most sensitive stage;
- all three EGWEE proxy-failure anchors share the same mechanism;
- external mechanism papers count as additional quantitative replications;
- the hidden-peak hypothesis is confirmed.
