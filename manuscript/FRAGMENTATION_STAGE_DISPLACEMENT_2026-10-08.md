# Fragmentation can relocate the limiting reproductive stage — evidence crosswalk (2026-10-08)

## Biological question

**Can a fragmented population retain high pollinator visitation and near-normal fruit/seed output while failing to recruit new individuals?**

Yes, in published species-level natural-history evidence. However, the underlying causes differ, and the evidence is not a synchronized multi-study dataset.

The critical contrast is not merely between interaction and female-fitness indices. It is between **short-term visible output** and **the actual demographic transition required for population persistence**.

## Myrtus communis: the strongest same-species sequence

### 2009: apparent early reproductive resilience (doi:10.1016/j.biocon.2009.01.017)

Six populations in fragmented Guadalquivir Valley woodland (approximately 1% residual forest cover) were compared by patch size. Small-patch visitation was highest and nearly monopolized by honeybees, while native wild bees were scarce. Seed brood size and mass were broadly similar between small and large patches. Small patches were not more pollen-limited for fruit set; some were less limited. This source already contributes to the frozen EGWEE qualitative map as QIF005 and to the SF06 accessible paired subset.

**Measurement ceiling:** visit/fruit/seed performance, not recruitment or population growth. Similar seed production does not mean the loss of native pollinator guilds is beneficial.

### 2010: connectivity-dependent offspring performance (doi:10.1111/j.1365-2664.2010.01879.x)

A separate study of seven regional populations classified habitats into large, small-connected and small-isolated groups. Controlled germination and seedling-performance measurements yielded approximately **61.9% normal surviving seedlings in large**, **66.9% in small-connected** and **48.3% in small-isolated** groups. The outcrossing rate (t_m), measured in a five-population genetic subset, differed strikingly despite the species having one nominal self-compatible mating system: **0.62 in small-connected, 0.35 in large and 0.13 in small-isolated** populations. Outcrossing rate correlated with cumulative germination and final seedling production (Kendall tau=1.0; p=0.014; n=5 populations), while historical adult genetic diversity was a weaker explanation in the source analysis.

This is **not a simple monotonic patch-area gradient**, nor does the self-compatibility label determine effective outcrossing. The small-connected group performed better than the small-isolated group despite both being small. Connectivity and contemporary mating patterns are biologically plausible drivers, but the five-population correlation is observational, small-n and not a causal experimental separation of connectivity from pollinator behavior. The seedling assay was conducted under controlled conditions, not a direct field recruitment estimate.

### 2012: actual field recruitment bottleneck (doi:10.1016/j.ppees.2011.11.002)

A four-year field programme across 10 woodland patches (four large and six small) quantified fruit production, fruit removal, bird-mediated seed rain, post-dispersal seed predation, emergence and seedling survival. Mean fruit set pooled across studied years was not significantly lower in small patches (approximately 52% small versus 43% large), yet recruitment probability fell when successive demographic transitions were integrated. Seedling emergence and especially seedling survival were major bottlenecks. Some small patches lacked saplings/juveniles, consistent with a persistent recruitment deficit. Poor habitat/microsite quality was a likely contributing condition.

### Provenance limit

The 2009, 2010 and 2012 papers study the same species in the Guadalquivir fragmented-woodland landscape but use different population sets and temporal frames. They cannot be row-joined as a synchronized Q→seed→recruitment time series. They are **same-species, same-landscape triangulation**, not three independent species-level replications. No additional effect enters the frozen EGWEE denominator.

## Cross-system contrast: the next stage that fails is not fixed

**Tristerix corymbosus** (doi:10.1111/1365-2745.12083 and doi:10.1016/j.biocon.2007.06.014). At forest edges hummingbird visitation and fruit set were largely retained, and pollen tubes tended to increase. Fruit removal near edges, however, was far lower than in forest interiors (greater than tenfold contrast), tracing the bottleneck to habitat-sensitive dispersers. Separate habitat comparisons linked disperser disappearance to failure of seed dispersal, poorer recruitment and juvenile deficits. The two publications are separate sites/years and must not be numerically joined.

**Samanea saman** (doi:10.1046/j.1523-1739.2002.00317.x). Isolated trees retained similar visible stigma pollen loads and undamaged-seed numbers, yet adequate pollen-tube receipt was rarer (0.4% versus 2.1% for flowers exceeding 23 pollen tubes); progeny were more related/inbred and showed lower germination and seedling vigor. A seed count can mask genetic/post-zygotic performance loss.

**Rhododendron ferrugineum** (doi:10.1186/s12862-014-0243-7). Facilitated selfing raised short-term seed production by about 27% on average across patches, but estimated lifetime inbreeding depression approached 0.9. A short-term assurance pathway need not sustain mature fitness.

## Why the SF06 SC/SI modifier can fail

The corrected SF06 fragmentation residual model did not resolve a self-compatibility coefficient after controlling pollination effect (gamma_SC=+0.0639; 95% CI -0.263 to +0.390; p=0.701). The Myrtus evidence gives a specific **within-species** reason a nominal SC label can be insufficient: effective outcrossing varies from a predominantly selfed isolated population to substantially outcrossed connected populations under the *same* compatibility classification, and outcrossing tracks seedling quality. This is mechanistic compatibility, not evidence that Myrtus caused the SF06 null or that all species behave similarly.

## Three falsifiable competing biological routes

1. **Mating-quality limit**: visitation is maintained, but compatible donor diversity or effective pollen declines. Prediction: outcross-pollen supplementation improves seed production or offspring quality in a matched episode.
2. **Downstream mutualist limit**: flowers and fruit set remain adequate while a disperser or another obligatory partner disappears. Prediction: disperser access or targeted seed placement changes seed rain/recruitment more than pollination augmentation, as suggested by Tristerix.
3. **Establishment-microsite limit**: seed output and arrival can remain adequate but conditions for germination and seedling survival are degraded. Prediction: standardized seed addition without microhabitat restoration fails to normalize recruitment; safe-site improvement has a larger effect, as suggested by Myrtus.

A fourth route, **short-term assurance followed by genetic filtering**, is distinguished by preserved seed number with high selfing/relatedness and lower later survival (Rhododendron and Samanea).

## Conservation implication

The appropriate intervention depends on **the first biological transition that fails**, not on the most accessible upstream indicator:

- Pollen shortage or provenance loss → effective pollination and mate connectivity.
- Disperser loss → disperser habitat, connectivity and seed-delivery routes.
- Recruitment microsite loss → germination substrate, shade/moisture and seedling establishment habitat.
- Inbreeding-filtered recruits → genetic quality and survival over a sufficient life stage.

The same visible early resilience can be produced by very different processes; fixing one common upstream proxy does not guarantee rescue of the actually limiting downstream stage.

## Novelty and inferential ceiling

These individual case findings were published previously, and sequential demographic bottlenecks are an established concept. EGWEE must not claim to have discovered seedling limitation, reproductive assurance or mutualist partner loss.

The contribution is a *source-graded synthesis*: the frozen I–F monitoring result and corrected SF06 external topology identify a reproducible proxy limitation, and the independently published life-stage examples show **concrete, distinguishable ecological explanations** that make different management predictions.

This evidence does **not** prove that stage relocation is the dominant cause across the 16 programmes or the 54 external consensus-sign pairs. The 2009/2010/2012 Myrtus results are not synchronized, and adult population viability/lambda was not directly established by the early-output studies. This note is mechanistic triangulation, not a new fitted causal law.
