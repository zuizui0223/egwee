## Purpose

The complete I–F census shows that every resolved mismatch between interaction quantity and reproductive function is F-dominant. This audit asks whether a comparable directional pattern appears when the upstream process is moved one step closer to realised movement or mating.

This is a **secondary descriptive audit**, not a new pooled meta-analysis and not fresh confirmation of the downstream-bottleneck hypothesis.

## Programme set

Four independent programmes currently provide a paired movement/connectivity or mating-support endpoint together with reproductive function under the same registered fragmentation frame:

1. ML001 *Serapias lingua* — pollen immigration (C) / fruit set (F);
2. ML002 *Brosimum alicastrum* — correlated paternity support (C) / progeny vigour (F);
3. ML014 *Eucalyptus socialis* — mating-support representation from correlated paternity (G_mating) / progeny growth (F);
4. P2_CF01_ACER_MIYABEI_2014 — seed-kinship connectivity support (C) / viable seed density (F).

The first three are direct Hedges-g programmes; Acer is a separate Fisher-z gradient programme. Their numerical effects are not pooled across families.

- Acer points weakly in the opposite direction but is unresolved.

## Ecological interpretation

This audit argues against a single fixed bottleneck position.

The I–F census contains resolved cases where reproductive function deteriorates more than interaction quantity. In contrast, the movement/mating-support audit contains a resolved *Serapias* case where pollen immigration deteriorates more than fruit set, and two additional systems point in the same direction without resolving the difference.

Thus fragmentation can plausibly produce at least three response geometries:

1. **coupled deterioration** — adjacent stages decline together;
2. **downstream function-dominant mismatch** — interaction quantity is buffered while F deteriorates;
3. **upstream movement/mating-dominant mismatch** — connectivity or mating support deteriorates while F is partly buffered.

The third geometry is compatible with reproductive compensation or buffering, but the current programmes do not identify a common compensation mechanism.

## Why this does not test the fresh effective-mating hypothesis

The fresh validation programme distinguishes `I_quantity` from a synchronized `I_effective / mating quality` endpoint within the same new I–F programme.

The four programmes here are not that validation set:

- they were already in the discovery corpus;
- their process endpoints span pollen immigration, correlated paternity and seed kinship;
- their F endpoints differ biologically;
- they belong to different registered effect families;
- none provides the prospective three-stage chain `I_quantity → I_effective → F` in the same fresh programme.

Therefore these data cannot be used to confirm or reject the fresh H2 prediction that a directly measured effective-mating layer may explain quantity–function decoupling.

## Strong ecological conclusion

> Fragmentation does not have one fixed life-cycle bottleneck. Current natural programmes contain both downstream function-dominant and upstream movement/mating-dominant mismatches, alongside coupled and unresolved regimes.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- bottleneck position is heterogeneous across the audited programmes;
- *Serapias* resolves a stronger connectivity than fruit-set response under its registered direct contrast;
- the existing movement/mating–F set is too small and heterogeneous for a cross-system law.

Not allowed:

- connectivity generally deteriorates before reproduction;
- reproductive compensation is demonstrated across systems;
- these four programmes validate the future quantity-versus-effective-mating hypothesis;
- Hedges-g and Fisher-z process differences can be pooled numerically.
