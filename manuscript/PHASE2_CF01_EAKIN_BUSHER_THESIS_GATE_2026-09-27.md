# CFTQ0319 Eakin-Busher urban plant-pollinator thesis gate — resolved 2026-09-27

## Programme

Programme identity: `P2_CF01_EAKIN_BUSHER_THESIS_2021`.

Umbrella source: Eakin-Busher (2021), *Flowers and bees in the city: The impact of urbanisation on
native plants and their insect pollinators*, Murdoch University PhD thesis.

The thesis is an urban-fragmentation source-discovery object, not one independent programme.

## Chapter mapping

The thesis declaration identifies three empirical chapters.

### Chapter 3

Remnant bushlands and residential gardens provide complementary insect resources/habitat.

This is an insect/landscape chapter and does not provide a common native-plant I/F fragmentation
response.

### Chapter 4

Published as Eakin-Busher et al. (2020), *Australian Journal of Botany*,
“Mating strategies dictate the importance of insect visits to native plants in urban fragments”,
doi:10.1071/BT19122.

The study:

- works with five native plant species in Perth urban fragments;
- manipulates insect access and pollen transfer;
- records floral visitors;
- measures reproductive success.

Its causal estimand is mating/pollination treatment and species dependence on insect visitors, not
one source-defined habitat-fragmentation severity variable shared across I and F.

Urban fragments are the ecological setting; they are not a replicated fragmented/reference effect
family for this chapter.

### Chapter 5

“Gene flow and pollination of a rhizomatous herb in a fragmented urban landscape.”

The thesis abstract states that *Patersonia occidentalis* paternity was influenced by mother–father
distance, with pollen movement up to **116 m** within a small remnant.

This is a valid within-remnant C/paternity result, but it is not a multi-remnant fragmentation
gradient and it does not share a direct F fragmentation response with Chapter 4.

## No rescue

Do not:

- count the thesis in addition to Chapter 4 as independent K;
- concatenate Chapter 4 mating experiments with Chapter 5 paternity;
- treat “urban fragment” as a quantitative fragmentation severity without a source estimand;
- treat mother/father distances inside one remnant as replicated fragmented landscapes;
- infer a direct F response from offspring paternity;
- combine the five Chapter-4 species as independent programmes.
