# CFTQ0162 Celtis forest-modification estimand gate — 2026-09-24

## Programme

Programme identity: `P2_CF01_CELTIS_MODIFIED_FORESTS_2013`.

Primary source: Neuschulz et al. (2013), *Austral Ecology* 38:374–382,
doi:10.1111/j.1442-9993.2012.02417.x.

The source measures flower visitation and fruit set of *Celtis africana* across 36 study sites in
six source-defined forest-modification types.

## Why the biological design is strong

The source provides:

- direct floral visitation;
- direct fruit set;
- two study years;
- broad replication across a heterogeneous human-modified landscape.

The six forest types are explicitly described as differing in human disturbance.

## Why EGWEE does not manufacture a fragmentation effect

The six categories combine qualitatively different dimensions:

1. continuous natural forest in nature reserves;
2. natural forest fragments in nature reserves;
3. forest fragments in eucalyptus plantations;
4. fragments in agricultural matrix;
5. forest gardens;
6. secondary forest in game reserves.

This is a **forest-modification factor**, not one source-supplied quantitative fragmentation axis.
The categories jointly change fragment status, matrix, management and successional history.

The peer-reviewed analysis reports responses by forest type. It does not provide one registered
numeric severity variable or one binary fragmented/reference contrast that can be applied to both I
and F without an EGWEE-side recoding choice.

Therefore EGWEE does not:

- assign equally spaced severity scores 1–6;
- infer distances between category levels;
- collapse continuous forest + natural fragments into a "natural" group and the remaining four
  types into a "modified" group after seeing the published directions;
- treat forest type labels as a continuous fragmentation gradient.

## Decision

Status: `close_no_single_response_free_fragmentation_estimand`.

Quantitative I-F programme increment: **0**.

This is not an ecological null. It is an estimand boundary.

## Process evidence retained

The source reports that flower visitation differs strongly among forest types and is enhanced in
human-modified forests, while fruit set is positively associated with visitation/diversity but only
weakly predicted by forest type.

That pattern is retained as a **process-decoupling / service-persistence anchor**: substantial
reorganization or even enhancement of the interaction layer does not imply a correspondingly large
change in realized reproductive function.

This process interpretation is useful for EGWEE, but it is not converted into a cross-layer effect
size without a source-defined common fragmentation estimand.

## No rescue

Do not:

- create a 1–6 ordinal fragmentation severity scale;
- create a post hoc natural-versus-modified binary contrast;
- choose category pooling because it maximizes I-F separation;
- use year, tree, flower, insect visit or fruit as fragmentation n;
- treat the process anchor as validation of an EGWE/NEE finite operator.
