## Key decisions

### CFTQ0032 / CFTQ0036 — bluebell isolation experiment

The published PLOS ONE paper (CFTQ0032) is a strong **F fragmentation-gradient** study:

- 51 woodland patches;
- response-free patch-area and proximity/isolation metrics;
- 153 phytometer pots;
- direct seed/capsule/quality reproductive traits.

However, the experiment does not independently measure visitor/pollination service. Pollination is inferred mechanistically from reproductive traits. Therefore it is not an I-F pair.

CFTQ0036 is the Research Square preprint of the same study and is linked as a duplicate publication, not a second programme.

### CFTQ0034 — Antirrhinum majus

This study has genuine C and F biology:

- six habitat patches separated by seawater;
- ten-year pedigree;
- functional connectivity between patches;
- reproductive success of migrant-derived versus resident plants.

It is retained as high-value **C-F mechanism/compensation evidence inside a fragmented landscape**.

It does not estimate how C and F change with fragmentation severity or a fragmented/reference exposure. Migrant status is the F comparison, so it cannot enter the fragmentation-response C-F programme denominator.

### CFTQ0038 — Sagittaria trifolia

This experiment jointly measures pollinator visitation, seed production, siring success and pollen-dispersal distance at population edges versus interiors.

The exposure is position within two experimental common-garden plant populations, not habitat-fragment edge/reference geometry. Population edge is therefore not retroactively equated with habitat fragmentation.

The programme is retained as mechanistic edge I/C/F evidence outside the natural fragmentation-response family.
