# Interaction–function measurement-gap audit — 2026-09-27

## Result

The complete registered I–F corpus contains **8 independent programmes**.

All **8/8** use an I endpoint that measures interaction or pollen **quantity** without directly identifying compatible mating quality on the same I–F frame.

Current I endpoints are:

| programme | I endpoint | classification |
|---|---|---|
| Chaco / ML020 | pollen tubes | quantity only |
| Sevenello | all bees per transect | quantity only |
| Kakamega | standardized visit occurrence | quantity only |
| *Eucalyptus wandoo* | pollen tubes at base of style | quantity only |
| *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* | pollinator abundance per flower | quantity only |
| Zurich BetterBlooms | all-pollinator capture rate per 9 h | quantity only |
| common milkweed | total pollinator abundance per surveyed plant | quantity only |
| Pritchard custard apple | total floral visitor abundance | quantity only |

No registered I–F programme directly pairs F with:

- compatible conspecific pollen identity/quality;
- realized paternity or effective pollen-donor diversity;
- an experimentally calibrated effective-pollination measure that already incorporates mating compatibility.

Total pollen tubes are retained as quantity-level evidence here because they measure successful pollen germination/growth quantity but do not identify compatible donor diversity or realized mating success.

## Why this matters ecologically

An I endpoint can be biologically close to pollination while still remaining upstream of successful reproduction.

The life-cycle chain is:

```
visitor / pollen quantity
        ↓
compatible pollen and effective mating
        ↓
fertilization / post-pollination filtering
        ↓
fruit and viable seed production
```

Thus a stable visit rate, pollinator abundance or pollen-tube count cannot by itself exclude a downstream reproductive bottleneck.

The current F-dominant direction census is therefore paired with a clear evidence gap: the intermediate effective-mating stage is not directly represented in any of the eight registered I–F programmes.

## Interpretation boundary

This audit does **not** show that all unresolved I–F programmes would decouple if compatible mating were measured.

It does show that the current literature corpus cannot distinguish, across programmes, whether:

1. I and F are genuinely coupled;
2. interaction quantity is maintained but mating quality fails;
3. interaction quantity itself is the limiting stage;
4. post-pollination resource or developmental filters dominate.

## Consequence for the fresh validation programme

The absence of effective-mating I endpoints is why the prospective field design explicitly measures:

`I_quantity → I_effective / mating quality → F`

The strongest future test is not simply to add more pollinator counts. It is to add a synchronized intermediate measure such as compatible pollen, realized paternity or effective pollen-donor diversity on the same independent sites.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

> The complete audited I–F corpus is rich in interaction-quantity measurements but contains no programme that directly measures effective mating quality alongside reproductive function on the same fragmentation frame.

Not allowed:

- effective mating quality is always the missing mechanism;
- pollen-tube measurements are biologically uninformative;
- adding paternity data would necessarily make every I–F mismatch significant;
- the 0/8 gap estimates the frequency of this measurement limitation in all fragmentation research.
