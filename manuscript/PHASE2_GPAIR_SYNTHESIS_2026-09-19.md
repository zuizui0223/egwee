# Phase-2 five-programme G_adult−G_offspring synthesis — 2026-09-19

## Result

The preregistered adult-versus-offspring genetic pair gate has reached **5 independent programmes**, so the cross-programme contrast can now be estimated.

Primary estimand:

`G_adult - G_offspring`

Positive values indicate that the adult standing genetic response to fragmentation is more positive / less negative than the offspring response.

The programme-level REML estimate is:

> **mean contrast = +0.129; modified Knapp–Hartung 95% CI = −0.655 to +0.912**

with:
- `tau² = 0`;
- Cochran `Q = 1.688` on 4 df;
- descriptive `I² = 0%`;
- `t_4 = 0.455`;
- two-sided `p = 0.672`.

The primary interval crosses zero widely. Therefore the current five-programme evidence does **not** resolve a common directional cohort lag.

This is not evidence that adult and offspring responses are exchangeable. It means that a shared directional difference is not identified at the current programme count and uncertainty.

## What changed scientifically

Before Phase 2, adult-versus-offspring response geometry was a Spondias-centered secondary hypothesis with insufficient cross-system replication.

It is now a directly estimable repeated contrast across five independent programmes:

1. ML003 — *Spondias purpurea*;
2. P2_SF05_93 — *Parkia biglobosa*;
3. P2_SF05_71 — *Heliconia acuminata*;
4. P2_CF01_GPAIR_002 — *Prunus africana* in Ethiopia;
5. P2_CF01_GPAIR_003 — *Prunus africana* in Kakamega.

The result is informative but negative with respect to a universal directional lag: the replicated systems do not currently support the simple rule that offspring genetic responses are systematically more negative than adult standing genetic responses under fragmentation.

## Spondias dependence rule

Spondias has two co-primary offspring stages. The primary programme estimate does not select one.

- adult minus juvenile contrast = `+2.2404`;
- adult minus seed contrast = `+0.1770`;
- their equal-weight covariance-aware programme contrast = `+1.2087`.

The juvenile-only five-programme sensitivity gives mean `+0.185`, mKH 95% CI `[-0.596, +0.967]`.

The seed-only sensitivity gives mean `+0.082`, mKH 95% CI `[-0.706, +0.870]`.

Both preserve the same zero-crossing conclusion.

## Influence

Leave-one-programme-out pooled point estimates range from approximately `-0.062` to `+0.257`.

No single programme omission creates a stable directional cohort-lag signal. In particular, dropping the high-precision Heliconia programme changes the pooled point estimate slightly negative but does not create a large common effect.

## Heterogeneity boundary

REML estimates `tau² = 0` and descriptive `I² = 0%`. With only five programmes, this is a boundary estimate and must **not** be interpreted as proof of biological homogeneity.

The modified Knapp–Hartung interval is retained as primary precisely because K is small. The narrower model-based normal 95% interval is `[-0.425, +0.682]` and is sensitivity only.

## Claim ceiling

What can now be said:

> Five independent natural fragmentation programmes permit a repeated adult-versus-offspring genetic response estimate, but they do not resolve a common directional cohort lag.

What cannot yet be said:
- adult and offspring responses are universally identical;
- fragmentation never produces cohort lag;
- time since fragmentation explains the between-system geometry;
- any moderator explains the variation;
- this pair result supersedes the frozen Journal of Ecology baseline.

Moderator models remain closed at K=5. Phase-2 supersession also remains blocked because the systematic search universe is not yet complete.
