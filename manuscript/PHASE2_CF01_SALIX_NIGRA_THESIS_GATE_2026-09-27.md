## Programme

Programme identity: `P2_CF01_SALIX_NIGRA_THESIS_2023`.

Primary thesis: Grady (2023), *The Birds and The Bees and The Willows: Understanding the
reproductive biology of Salix nigra Marsh.*, doi:10.33915/etd.12125.

## Chapter-level result

The thesis paternity study is a valid pollen-gene-flow analysis inside one defined *Salix nigra*
population.

It reports:

- assigned paternities up to **510 m** from maternal trees;
- about **12%** of offspring assigned to fathers outside the sampled candidate population;
- strong effects of mother–father distance on paternity.

These are useful C/movement observations.

However, the study does not define replicated fragmented and reference populations or a quantitative
habitat-fragmentation gradient. The statement that long-distance pollination may connect fragmented
populations is a conservation implication, not the source exposure.

## Linked pollinator paper

The associated *Salix nigra* flower-visitor study characterizes floral insects across years, tree
sexes and chemical/visual traits in **one population**. It does not supply a habitat-fragmentation
contrast that can be paired with the thesis gene-flow study.
