## Most informative closures

### CFTQ0241 — Maianthemum bifolium

The paper is genuinely about old fragmented forest populations and links fruiting success to genetic
state and mate availability. However, the directly fitted reproductive axes are:

- genotypic diversity;
- number of nearby conspecific populations;
- within-population versus nearby-population hand-pollen source.

Those are biologically relevant consequences of historical fragmentation, but they are not one
source-defined habitat-fragmentation exposure shared by interaction and reproductive endpoints.

Decision: retain as a clonality / mate-limitation / genetic-diversity mechanism anchor; no direct
fragmentation I-F programme.

### CFTQ0243 — Stenocereus stellatus

The source has strong direct I-F measurements across wild, managed-in-situ and cultivated
populations: bat visitation, pollen receipt and fruit set.

The source contrast is nevertheless **management/domestication state**, not habitat fragmentation.
EGWEE does not convert wild–managed–cultivated classes into a fragmentation gradient after seeing
the pollination response.

Decision: no habitat-fragmentation I-F programme.

### CFTQ0245 — Lysimachia asperulifolia

The study identifies several mechanisms limiting reproduction:

- rare/ineffective pollinator visitation;
- pollen fertility;
- self-incompatibility / S-allele diversity;
- restricted pollen movement;
- prescribed-fire effects.

Habitat fragmentation is an important conservation context and limits among-population movement, but
the paper does not provide one replicated fragmentation exposure on which I and F are jointly
estimated.

Decision: mechanism evidence only; no fragmentation I-F programme.
