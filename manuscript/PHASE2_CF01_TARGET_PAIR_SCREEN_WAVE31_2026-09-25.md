## CFTQ0301 — Spondias mombin

Nason, Aldrich & Hamrick report original empirical fragmentation data for *Spondias mombin*.

The source shows a biologically striking combination:

- small island fragments have reduced fruit production / germination;
- approximately **90–100%** of progeny in small fragments are attributed to pollen immigration;
- inferred pollen sources can occur roughly **80–1000 m** away.

This is therefore a strong direct C-F decoupling candidate: reproductive function declines while
long-distance pollen connectivity remains active.

A retrospective effect-unit gate is frozen before numerical recovery:

- primary fragmented condition = small island fragments;
- primary reference = continuous-forest populations FDP + LC;
- larger island/fragment DL = context/sensitivity only;
- C = population-level pollen immigration / gene-flow support;
- F = population-level fruit production;
- individual trees, fruits, seeds and progeny are nested below population;
- five Ficus species in the same article are C-only case studies and do not add C-F programme K.

The current public reporting is not yet enough for direct Hedges-g admission because C is summarized
at population level while fruit production is largely shown from individual-tree/DBH data.

Status:
`pending_common_population_CF_effect_unit_and_fruit_production_recovery`.

The study is retained as a movement-compensation / reproductive-decline anchor even if quantitative
admission remains blocked.
