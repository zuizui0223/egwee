# Seed literature for the EGWEE multilayer fragmentation meta-analysis

These sources define the starting search corpus. They are not treated as independent evidence if they reuse the same primary studies; all primary studies must be deduplicated by DOI/title/species/system before quantitative synthesis.

## Prior quantitative syntheses

1. Aguilar et al. (2006), *Ecology Letters* 9:968–980, doi:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2006.00927.x — fragmentation effects on pollination and plant reproductive success.
2. Aguilar et al. (2008), *Molecular Ecology* 17:5177–5188, doi:10.1111/j.1365-294X.2008.03971.x — fragmentation effects on plant genetic diversity and mating-related genetic responses.
3. Aguilar et al. (2019), *Ecology Letters* 22:1163–1173, doi:10.1111/ele.13272 — progeny genetic erosion and progeny performance under fragmentation.
4. Méndez-Rojas et al. (2023), *AoB PLANTS* 15:plad019 — fine-scale spatial genetic structure under anthropogenic fragmentation/degradation.
5. Updated land-use meta-analysis in *Annals of Botany* (2024 online / 2025 volume) — pollination plus male and female fitness, with Supplementary Data Table S1 containing the meta-analysis dataset.
6. Olhnuud et al. (2025), *Journal of Applied Ecology*, doi:10.1111/1365-2664.70161 — insect-pollinator abundance/richness responses to fragmentation; deposited data: Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.dz08kps9p.

## Repository-audited mechanistic seed systems

The following systems are already documented in `background/` and should be searched back to their original primary papers. Inclusion is conditional on the same eligibility rules as every other study.

- Montpellier *Crepis sancta* — interaction limitation, reproductive assurance, pollen/seed movement and genetics across related studies.
- Miyake-jima *Camellia japonica–Zosterops japonicus* — volcanic resource loss, bird movement, pollen flow, reproductive function and offspring genetics.
- Perth *Conospermum undulatum* — reproduction, contemporary pollen flow and historical/adult genetic state.
- *Spondias purpurea* — visitation, reproductive success, realised pollen flow and cohort-specific genetics in continuous versus fragmented forest.
- Honshu–Izu coastal plant–pollinator networks — interaction/trait/function responses along island/mainland geography; likely secondary-gradient stream rather than automatic primary fragmentation contrast.
- Zurich BetterBlooms — urban land-use context, interaction guilds and reproductive function; screen against the strict fragmentation-exposure rule.
- Chicago *Penstemon hirsutus* green roofs — connectivity/function-rich urban patch system; screen original papers for extractable paired effects.
- fragmented *Primula elatior* — population size, morph/trait state, pollinators, reproduction and genetics; high-value multilayer candidate.
- *Oenothera harringtonii*, *Eschscholzia californica*, Mallorca carob and *Campanula americana* — retain as search seeds but do not assume that their existing EGWEE gate analyses meet the new fragmentation/meta-analysis inclusion criteria.

## Discovery rule

For each prior synthesis:

1. recover its study-level dataset or reference list;
2. normalise DOI/title/species names;
3. join candidate primary studies across synthesis domains;
4. flag studies that appear in two or more response domains;
5. return to the original paper/supplement for independent effect extraction;
6. extend citation and keyword searches forward through 2026-09-11.

The strongest first-pass candidates are studies appearing in more than one existing synthesis because they can provide the within-study cross-layer contrasts that are the paper's primary target.
