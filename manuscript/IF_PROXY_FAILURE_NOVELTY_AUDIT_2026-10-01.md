# Interaction–function proxy-failure novelty audit — 2026-10-01

## Purpose

Define the strongest defensible ecological novelty after the estimand-scale audit, sign-geometry audit and uncertainty audit.

## What is already established

### 1. Fragmentation can reduce both pollination and reproduction

Aguilar et al. (2006) and the recent update by Aguilar et al. (2025) already establish negative average effects of fragmentation / land-use change on pollination and reproductive fitness, together with a positive cross-species association between pollination and female-fitness effects.

Therefore this manuscript does not claim novelty for linking fragmentation, pollination and reproduction.

### 2. Species-specific fragmentation responses are already known

Dauber et al. (2010) examined flower visitation and seed set across 10 wild plant species in five European countries and explicitly reported strong plant-species-specific responses to habitat fragmentation and flowering-patch structure.

Therefore the present manuscript does not claim that species-specific visitation or seed-set responses are newly discovered.

### 3. Visitation is already known to be an imperfect proxy for pollination effectiveness

King et al. (2013) showed that visitation data can misrepresent pollinator effectiveness and that direct single-visit pollen deposition gives different information. Subsequent work has likewise shown that more visitors need not imply greater reproductive output.

Therefore 'visitation is not enough' is not itself the novel result.

## What the present synthesis adds

### A. Complete matched fragmentation-specific denominator

The registered interaction–function corpus contains **8 independent programmes** and **18 primary panels** in which an interaction/pollen-quantity response and reproductive function are evaluated under the same registered landscape exposure.

All eight programme-level I endpoints are quantity-level measures; none directly measures effective mating quality on the same I–F frame.

### B. Representation-stable resolved proxy failure is directionally asymmetric

Three independent programmes have a resolved downstream I–F mismatch that remains stable under their audited representation:

- *Eucalyptus wandoo* — Western Australia, Myrtaceae;
- *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* — Brazilian cerrado, Annonaceae;
- Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens* — western Kenya, Acanthaceae.

These programmes span three continents, three plant families, different growth forms and sharply different pollination/mating contexts.

In all three, reproductive function is more negatively affected than the measured interaction/pollen-quantity endpoint.

No programme in the audited I–F corpus has an equally representation-stable resolved upstream mismatch in which interaction quantity is more negatively affected than reproductive function.

This is a descriptive directional asymmetry, not a sign/binomial test and not a prevalence estimate.

### C. Point-sign topology is broader but weaker

Across 18 primary panels:

- opposite I/F point-estimate signs: 6 panels;
- independent programmes containing at least one opposite-sign panel: 5;
- both endpoint directions individually resolved in opposite directions at 95%: 0;
- one endpoint resolved and the other unresolved: 2;
- both endpoints unresolved: 4.

Thus the six point-sign reversals are useful qualitative topology, but they are not six independently resolved sign reversals.

### D. Same-exposure multi-species heterogeneity is descriptive context, not the novelty by itself

Among four multi-panel programmes sharing one registered exposure frame, three contain more than one point-sign topology among focal species/panels.

This supports the view that landscape exposure alone does not determine interaction→function translation. However, species-specific fragmentation responses are already well established in the literature, so this pattern is supporting context rather than the central novelty claim.

## Strongest ecological lead

> **When matched interaction quantity and reproductive function separate clearly under fragmentation, the stable repeated mismatch in the current audited corpus is one-sided: reproductive function is worse than the interaction-quantity proxy.**

This supports a practical ecological inference:

> **Interaction quantity can provide false reassurance about reproductive function under fragmentation.**

The novelty is the cross-program, fragmentation-specific, scale-audited recurrence of that failure mode—not the general idea that visitors differ in pollination effectiveness.

## Mechanistic interpretation

The three stable downstream programmes do not share one demonstrated mechanism.

Source biology nevertheless points to a common missing layer between interaction quantity and reproductive output:

- pollen compatibility / self versus outcross pollen;
- realised pollen donor diversity;
- pollinator effectiveness / visitor identity;
- post-pollination filtering;
- direct landscape or resource effects on reproductive output.

The current eight-programme I–F corpus measures none of these effective-mating variables consistently on the same frame.

Therefore the next prospective design should measure:

`interaction quantity → effective mating / compatible pollen → reproductive function`

with effect scale declared before outcomes are opened.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- three independent programmes provide representation-stable downstream interaction–function mismatches;
- those three programmes span three continents and three plant families;
- no representation-stable resolved upstream I–F programme is present in the current audited corpus;
- six opposite-sign point-estimate panels occur across five programmes, but none has both endpoint directions individually resolved at 95%;
- three of four multi-panel programmes show more than one point-sign topology under one registered exposure frame;
- interaction quantity is insufficient as a stand-alone sentinel of reproductive function;
- fresh tests should measure effective mating / interaction quality, not quantity alone.

Not allowed:

- 3/8 is a global prevalence estimate;
- a binomial/sign test on the three stable downstream programmes;
- all fragmented plants show hidden reproductive loss;
- reverse `I−/F+` compensation is absent in nature;
- species-specific fragmentation response is newly discovered;
- flower visitation being an imperfect pollination proxy is newly discovered;
- one pollen-quality mechanism explains all three programmes.

## Closest literature boundary

- Aguilar et al. 2006 / 2025: average negative coupling of pollination and reproductive fitness across species;
- Dauber et al. 2010: strong plant-species-specific visitation and seed-set responses to fragmentation;
- King et al. 2013: flower visitation is a poor proxy for pollination effectiveness;
- Burns et al. 2022: individual-community evidence that fewer or different visitors can coexist with equal or greater reproductive output.

## Writing implication

Lead the paper with scale-stable direction in the primary five-cluster stream and the repeated downstream interaction–function proxy-failure pattern in the broader matched corpus.

Keep the six opposite-sign panels and within-programme species heterogeneity as descriptive, hypothesis-generating support.

Do not use 'species-specific translation' as the sole novelty claim.
