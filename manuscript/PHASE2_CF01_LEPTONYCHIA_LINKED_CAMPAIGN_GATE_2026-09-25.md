# CFTQ0215 Leptonychia linked-campaign C-F gate — resolved 2026-09-27

## Programme identity

Programme: `P2_CF01_LEPTONYCHIA_2003_2009`.

Primary publications:

- Cordeiro & Howe (2003), *PNAS*, forest fragmentation and seed-disperser mutualism;
- Cordeiro et al. (2009), *Ecology*, disperser limitation and recruitment.

The 2009 paper explicitly treats the 2003 dispersal result as prior evidence and asks whether other
processes—especially fecundity, seed quality, predation or abiotic recruitment limitation—can
explain the same fragmentation-associated recruitment failure.

## Resolved 2009 source frame

The public 2009 Appendix A now resolves the principal landscape identities as:

- small fragments: **2, 9, 16 and 31 ha**;
- continuous-forest localities: **520 and 3500 ha**.

A **21-ha** fragment is explicitly identified as being used only for the reciprocal-transplant
experiment and is not added to the primary C/F frame.

This is not an exact copy of the earlier 2003 four-fragment description, so the two campaigns cannot
be silently treated as one synchronized effect unit.

## What Appendix A actually exposes

The fragment-level tables in Appendix A are:

- Table A2 — rodent abundance by fragment area and year;
- Table A3 — dove abundance by fragment area and census period.

Those are biologically relevant process/context variables, but they are **not direct seed movement /
seed-removal C** under the frozen EGWEE layer definition.

The appendix also reports 2002 fruit-crop information across **238 trees** and describes the crop-size
estimation method. It does not expose realised fecundity as a matching vector for the same
2, 9, 16, 31, 520 and 3500-ha habitat units.

Trees are nested biological observations, not independent forest fragments.

## Why C-F is not admissible

The public source audit now establishes site identity but still fails the common-effect-unit gate:

1. the direct seed-dispersal C evidence is established primarily in the linked 2003 programme and
   extended qualitatively/observationally in 2009;
2. the 2009 public appendix does not provide a direct six-unit seed-movement vector;
3. the 2009 crop/fecundity information is not reported as a matching six-unit fragmentation-level
   F vector;
4. the 2003 and 2009 habitat frames/campaign windows cannot be concatenated solely by species and
   landscape identity.

Same species + same region + linked citations are not sufficient to create a paired multilayer
cluster.

## Terminal status

`leptonychia_public_data_no_common_CF_frame`

Direct C-F increment = **0**.

The programme is retained as strong **dispersal-limitation / mechanism evidence**. The source-level
interpretation is that reduced fecundity, seed quality, predation and abiotic seedling performance
did not explain the fragment recruitment deficit, leaving reduced seed dispersal as the strongest
supported bottleneck.

That mechanism interpretation is not converted into a covariance-aware C-F effect.

## Reopening condition

Reopen only if an authoritative source supplies either:

- matching fragmentation-unit direct seed-movement and realised-fecundity values from one campaign;
  or
- an explicit cross-campaign unit/dependence map permitting the 2003/2009 observations to be linked
  without treating trees, watches, seeds, seedlings or transplants as landscape replicates.

## No rescue

Do not:

- use rodent or dove abundance as direct C;
- pair 2003 C with 2009 F merely because the species and landscape are the same;
- use pooled tree-level crop dispersion as fragment-level F variance;
- count 238 trees as fragmentation n;
- add the 21-ha transplant-only fragment to the primary frame;
- use recruitment as F;
- digitize a figure to invent the missing common C/F vector;
- interpret failure of the fecundity alternative as statistical C-F equivalence;
- count the 2003 and 2009 papers as two independent programmes.
