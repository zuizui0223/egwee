# Reviewer-risk audit — variable life-cycle bottleneck synthesis
> **Status after the estimand-scale audit (2026-09-27): exploratory registered-scale record only.**
> Hedges-g and oriented-lnRR sensitivities change relative response ordering and robustness classification. This document is retained for provenance and hypothesis generation, but its bottleneck/separation classifications are not scale-invariant submission-headline evidence. See `manuscript/ESTIMAND_SCALE_SENSITIVITY_RESULT_2026-09-27.md`.


## Purpose

Red-team the current Journal of Ecology framing before submission and define the maximum defensible response to likely reviewer objections.

## Risk 1 — “Multiprocess fragmentation is already known”

**Likely objection:** Previous fragmentation syntheses already integrate multiple processes, life stages and response types.

**Evidence:** Ibáñez et al. (2014) explicitly synthesized multiple fragmentation processes and plant responses/life stages. Aguilar et al. (2006, 2025) established average pollination and reproductive effects and positive pollination–fitness association.

**Response:** Agree. The manuscript does not claim novelty for multiprocess fragmentation itself. Its contribution is the within-programme paired estimand: process-specific responses are compared under the same fragmentation exposure and independent-unit frame while retaining their dependence.

**Status:** addressed in Introduction and novelty audit.

## Risk 2 — “The 12-programme census is post hoc”

**Likely objection:** The unified census was constructed after individual programme results existed.

**Response:** Correct. It is labelled a post hoc descriptive integration and is never used for a pooled p-value, sign test, common effect or prevalence estimate. Each programme retains its pre-existing dependence and multiplicity rule. The only manuscript-level use is to ask whether the audited corpus contains resolved mismatch in more than one direction.

**Status:** addressed in Methods, Limitations and freeze claim ceiling.

## Risk 3 — “Hedges-g and Fisher-z are incomparable”

**Likely objection:** Direct contrasts and continuous gradients use different effect families.

**Response:** They are never numerically pooled in the 12-programme census. Direction is assigned only after a within-programme comparison on that programme's registered scale. Table 2 is a response-geometry/status census, not a common-effect meta-analysis.

**Status:** machine-checked.

## Risk 4 — “Bottleneck implies causality”

**Likely objection:** A larger standardized fragmentation response does not identify a causal or demographic rate-limiting stage.

**Response:** The manuscript defines bottleneck position operationally as the member of a paired process–F comparison with the more negative standardized fragmentation response when the difference is resolved. It explicitly disclaims causal mediation, temporal order and unique demographic limitation.

**Status:** addressed in Introduction, Methods and canonical census note.

## Risk 5 — “Variable bottleneck position depends on one system”

**Likely objection:** Only *Serapias lingua* supplies a resolved upstream process-dominant mismatch.

**Response:** Correct. Leave-one-programme-out auditing shows that omitting ML001 removes the only resolved upstream direction; the remaining three resolved programmes are all downstream F-dominant. The title and synthesis therefore use **evidence for variable bottleneck positions**, and the manuscript states that bidirectional bottleneck variation is not leave-one-programme-out robust.

**Status:** machine-checked and promoted to the main claim ceiling.

## Risk 6 — “The evidence universe is not an exhaustive systematic review”

**Likely objection:** Candidate recovery was targeted rather than a full all-database systematic review.

**Response:** Agree. The manuscript limits inference to the audited corpus. Phase-2 target-pair screening is complete at 360/360 under its frozen search universe, but unseeded literature outside that universe may exist. No global regime prevalence is estimated.

**Status:** explicit limitation.

## Risk 7 — “Unresolved does not mean coupled”

**Likely objection:** Failure to resolve a difference is not evidence of equality.

**Response:** Correct. The unified census labels seven programmes `unresolved_process_function_difference`; Figure 4 no longer equates unresolved with coupled. Chaco and Sevenello may be described as coupled-looking at current precision only when their joint direction is biologically relevant.

**Status:** encoded in CSV, Table 2 and Figure 4.

## Risk 8 — “The primary global Fisher result is Serapias-driven”

**Likely objection:** Omitting ML001 changes the five-cluster combined p from 0.0121 to 0.1819.

**Response:** This is reported in Abstract, Results, Discussion, Figure 2 and the submission claim ceiling. The final ecological conclusion does not rely on the global Fisher result alone; it is paired with the complete process–function census and influence analysis. But the upstream bottleneck direction is also ML001-dependent, so *Serapias* remains an influential system in both inference layers.

**Status:** fully disclosed.

## Risk 9 — “Why call this a meta-analysis?”

**Likely objection:** Plant-ecology meta-analysis standards require appropriate effect weighting, search transparency, sensitivity analysis and available data rather than vote counting.

**Response:** The primary direct synthesis uses standardized effects and sampling variances, independent-unit rules, explicit covariance/dependence, cluster-level multiplicity, leave-one-cluster-out influence, zero-covariance sensitivity and covariance-free certification. The 12-programme census is deliberately labelled descriptive and not presented as a pooled meta-analysis. Analysis-ready evidence and reproduction code are included in the anonymous package.

**Context:** Koricheva & Gurevitch (2014) emphasize search reporting, sensitivity analysis, methodological assumptions and data availability as core quality issues for plant-ecology meta-analysis.

## Risk 10 — “Why not fit moderators now?”

**Likely objection:** If bottleneck position varies, test mating system or pollinator specialization as moderators.

**Response:** The current independent-programme denominator is too small and the resolved upstream direction is single-system. Fitting moderators after observing the 3:1 resolved geometry would invite overfitting and outcome-dependent coding. Moderator hypotheses are therefore frozen for genuinely fresh programmes under the H2-v2 validation contract.

**Status:** prospective only.

## Submission recommendation

The strongest defensible framing is:

> **Evidence for variable life-cycle bottleneck positions under habitat fragmentation, with substantial system influence and no claim about regime prevalence.**

The paper should not be sold as discovering multiprocess fragmentation, a universal downstream bottleneck, or a general causal mechanism.
