# CFTQ0215 Leptonychia 2003–2009 C-F recovery gate — 2026-09-27

## Programme

Programme identity: `P2_CF01_LEPTONYCHIA_2003_2009`.

Primary 2009 source: Cordeiro et al. (2009), *Ecology* 90:1030–1041,
doi:10.1890/07-1208.1.

Linked 2003 source: Cordeiro & Howe (2003), *PNAS*,
doi:10.1073/pnas.2331023100.

The biological programme is compelling: earlier fragmentation work established reduced disperser
activity / seed dispersal in small fragments, and the 2009 paper explicitly tested whether reduced
fecundity, seed quality, predation or abiotic conditions could instead explain poor recruitment.

The 2009 article concludes that the alternative explanations were not supported and that reduced
seed dispersal remained the most plausible mechanism.

## Public Appendix A audit

Figshare collection `3300914` contains one public appendix file,
`appendix-A.htm` (article id `3530888`).

The appendix resolves the 2009 landscape much better than the abstract alone.

### Habitat units

Figure A1 identifies the principal programme localities as:

- fragments: **2, 9, 16 and 31 ha**;
- continuous forest: **520 and 3500 ha**.

A **21-ha** fragment is identified as being used only in the reciprocal-transplant experiment and is
not silently added to the primary programme frame.

This is useful provenance, but it also shows that the 2009 frame is not simply copied from the linked
2003 four-fragment description.

### Appendix tables

The tabular fragment-level data in Appendix A are:

- Table A2 — rodent abundance by fragment area and year;
- Table A3 — dove abundance by fragment area and census period.

These are predator / bird-abundance process measurements. They are **not** a direct six-unit
seed-movement / seed-removal C vector under the frozen EGWEE definition.

### Fecundity information

Appendix A states that 2002 fruit crops ranged from 2 to 8412 seeds and gives a pooled crop-size
summary across **238 trees**. It also describes the branch-counting procedure used to estimate crop
size.

However, the public appendix does not expose realised crop / seed-production values on the same
2, 9, 16, 31, 520 and 3500-ha habitat-unit frame required for a fragmentation-level F effect.

Trees are nested biological observations and cannot be promoted to six independent landscapes by
relabeling tree-level crop dispersion as fragment-level replication.

## Common-frame decision

The public source therefore resolves **site identity** but not a synchronized C-F effect unit.

A direct C-F programme would require matching fragmentation-unit values for:

1. direct seed movement / seed removal C;
2. realised fecundity / seed production F;
3. the same habitat units or an explicit source-supported cross-campaign dependence map.

Those conditions are not met by the public appendix.

The 2003 dispersal result and 2009 fecundity test cannot be concatenated merely because they involve
the same species and East Usambara landscape.

## Terminal status

`leptonychia_public_data_no_common_CF_frame`

Direct C-F programme increment: **0**.

The programme is retained as a **linked dispersal-limitation mechanism anchor**:

> local recruitment failure is not explained by reduced fecundity, seed quality, seed predation or
> seedling habitat performance; reduced seed dispersal remains the strongest supported bottleneck.

This mechanism interpretation is source-backed, but it is not converted into a covariance-aware
C-F meta-analytic contrast.

## Reopening condition

Reopen only if an authoritative source supplies either:

- matching fragmentation-unit direct seed-movement and realised-fecundity values from one campaign;
  or
- an explicit linked-campaign table that identifies the same fragmentation units and permits the
  2003/2009 dependence structure to be represented without treating trees, watches, seeds or
  seedlings as landscape replicates.

## No rescue

Do not:

- use rodent or dove abundance as direct C;
- use pooled tree-level crop SD as fragment-level variance;
- count 238 trees as fragmentation n;
- add the 21-ha transplant-only fragment to the primary frame;
- join 2003 C to 2009 F solely by species/system identity;
- digitize a figure to invent the missing common C/F vector;
- interpret failure of the fecundity alternative as statistical C-F equivalence;
- use the programme to validate an EGWE/NEE finite operator.
