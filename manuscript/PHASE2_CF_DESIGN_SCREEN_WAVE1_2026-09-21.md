## Why the first eight close

The common pattern is that the SF05 bibliography correctly flags movement/connectivity biology, but the same paper does not also provide an eligible direct reproductive-function endpoint.

- **CFQ001 Olea:** pollen immigration/paternity and SGS in one undisturbed population; no F and no direct fragmentation contrast.
- **CFQ002 Milicia:** replicated habitat populations and gene-dispersal/SGS information, but no fruit/seed/reproductive-function endpoint.
- **CFQ003 Carapa/Sextonia:** pollen dispersal and mating from mother/seed genotypes; no direct F and no replicated fragmentation frame.
- **CFQ004 Baillonella:** selfing, pollen dispersal and cohort inbreeding; inferred loss of inbred individuals is not recoded as a direct F endpoint.
- **CFQ005 Erythrophleum:** three density populations with C/mating endpoints; the paper explicitly treats pollen limitation of seed production as future work rather than a measured endpoint.
- **CFQ006 Hardy multi-species:** indirect gene-dispersal/FSGS comparison only.
- **CFQ007 Schinus:** genetic structure and internal gene flow across nine populations; no direct reproductive function.
- **CFQ008 Sorbus:** gene flow/recruitment history inside one 472-ha population; no direct F and no independent fragmentation reference.

## Consequence

This wave does not change direct C-F coverage, which remains **2/5 (ML001 + ML002)**.

The negative screen is informative: C-rich population-genetic studies are abundant, but same-programme reproductive-function measurements are much rarer. The remaining queue should therefore be screened for explicit fruit/seed/recruitment output before any numerical extraction is attempted.
