# Cover letter draft — Journal of Ecology

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit the Research Article **“Habitat fragmentation across plant reproductive life cycles: species-specific interaction–function translation and scale-sensitive amplitudes”** for consideration in *Journal of Ecology*.

Habitat-fragmentation syntheses usually ask whether endpoint classes decline on average. Our manuscript asks a matched plant-ecology question: when interaction and reproductive-function responses are observed under the same landscape exposure, do they move in the same direction, and do focal plant species translate the same perturbation into the same functional outcome?

We assembled five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters comprising 17 historical Hedges-g marginal effects, with explicit within-system dependence and effect-unit rules. On that registered estimand, the pooled five-cluster synthesis rejects complete equality of standardized response magnitudes (`p=0.0121`) and omission of *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.1819`. A mandatory post hoc lnRR audit shows that this robustness classification is scale-dependent: omission of *Serapias* remains significant under raw-unit multivariate delta covariance (`p=8.31×10^-5`) and zero covariance (`p=0.00434`). In *Serapias* itself, C–F is resolved on g but unresolved on lnRR (`p=0.6364`). We therefore retain the g analysis for reproducibility but do not present its separation/robustness pattern as a scale-general ecological conclusion.

The scale audit changed the manuscript's centre of gravity. Across the primary direct stream, all 17 oriented effects are negative on both g and lnRR, but relative response magnitude is scale-sensitive. We therefore separated magnitude inference from a post hoc qualitative sign audit of the complete matched I–F evidence. Across 18 primary panels in eight independent programmes, six panels in five programmes have opposite I/F signs, and both I+,F− and I−,F+ geometries occur. More strikingly, three of four multi-panel programmes contain different sign geometries among focal species sharing the same registered landscape/exposure frame. Individual studies have already shown that visitation can fail to predict reproduction; our added contribution is the cross-programme matched census showing that the qualitative translation from interaction to reproductive function itself varies among plant species and programmes. The repeated hidden-function-loss cases further show why interaction quantity can be an insufficient stand-alone functional proxy.

The manuscript is centred on plant ecology and plant–pollinator/reproductive processes, while integrating mating and genetic responses within the same biological systems. We therefore believe it fits *Journal of Ecology*’s interest in research that advances general understanding of plant populations and their interactions with other organisms.

The reviewer-facing manuscript has been prepared for double-anonymous review. Analysis-ready evidence tables and minimal reproduction code are assembled into an anonymised review package that reproduces the five-cluster synthesis, leave-one-out diagnostics, authoritative g-versus-lnRR scale audit, complete I–F sign-geometry census, exploratory registered-scale process–function census, main figures and main tables.
