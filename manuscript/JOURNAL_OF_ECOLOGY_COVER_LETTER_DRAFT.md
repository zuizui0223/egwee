# Cover letter draft — Journal of Ecology

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit the Research Article **“Pollination tracks but does not diagnose plant reproductive decline under habitat fragmentation”** for consideration in *Journal of Ecology*.

Habitat-fragmentation syntheses usually ask whether pollination and reproduction decline on average. Our manuscript asks a stricter indicator question: even when pollination genuinely responds to fragmentation and is statistically associated with female fitness, is its observed state sufficient to diagnose reproductive impairment in a focal plant system?

We assembled five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters comprising 17 historical Hedges-g marginal effects, with explicit within-system dependence and effect-unit rules. On that registered estimand, the pooled five-cluster synthesis rejects complete equality of standardized response magnitudes (`p=0.0121`) and omission of *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.1819`. A mandatory post hoc lnRR audit shows that this robustness classification is scale-dependent: omission of *Serapias* remains significant under raw-unit multivariate-delta covariance (`p=8.31×10^-5`) and zero covariance (`p=0.00434`). We therefore retain the registered g analysis for reproducibility but do not present its magnitude ordering as a scale-general ecological conclusion.

The scale-robust ecological result is different. All 17 primary direct effects deteriorate on both g and lnRR, yet matched interaction→function trajectories are many-to-many. Three independent quantitative programmes—*Eucalyptus wandoo*, *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* and Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens*—provide representation-stable downstream false-reassurance/severity mismatches across three continents and three plant families.

We then froze a broader 16-programme interaction→function map. Its many-to-many structure survives single-programme deletion and conservative category restrictions. More importantly, an external reanalysis of the accessible Aguilar et al. public Supplementary Table S1 independently generalizes the result: among 54 usable habitat-fragmentation pollination–female-fitness sign pairs, the best deterministic pollination-sign lookup leaves 12 mismatches; after removing every publication overlapping the frozen EGWEE map, 31 source-disjoint pairs still leave 7 mismatches, robust to publication and species deletion.

The external result is counterintuitive because association remains real. Aguilar et al. report a positive cross-species pollination–female-fitness association (`r=0.421`, `p<0.01`, `n=82`). Within the public-S1 fragmentation subset, the pollination slope is positive under both pair weighting (`beta=0.190`, `p=0.0053`) and equal-total-publication weighting (`beta=0.200`, `p=0.104`), making the direction stable but the subset-level significance weighting-sensitive. Yet pollination sign does not reduce binary classification error beyond the high baseline risk of reproductive decline, and leave-one-publication-out severity prediction improves mean-squared error by only about 2–4%. Thus a biological process can track downstream function on average while remaining an insufficient stand-alone sentinel of downstream state.

We keep the mechanism prospective. All eight registered quantitative I–F programmes measure interaction/pollen quantity and none directly measures compatible mating quality on the same frame. Source biology points to transfer provenance, effective mating, post-transfer viability and reproductive assurance as missing coordinates, but the manuscript does not claim one universal hidden mechanism.

Quantity–quality distinctions and imperfect visitation proxies are established concepts. Our contribution is narrower: a fragmentation-specific empirical demonstration, across matched programmes and a source-publication-disjoint external dataset, that average ecological coupling does not guarantee state-diagnostic or transferable predictive sufficiency.

The manuscript is centred on plant ecology and plant–pollinator/reproductive processes, while integrating mating and genetic responses within the same biological systems. We therefore believe it fits *Journal of Ecology*’s interest in research that advances general understanding of plant populations and their interactions with other organisms.

The reviewer-facing manuscript has been prepared for double-anonymous review. Analysis-ready evidence tables and minimal reproduction code are assembled into an anonymised review package that reproduces the five-cluster synthesis, leave-one-out diagnostics, authoritative g-versus-lnRR scale audit, complete I–F sign-geometry census, frozen 16-programme interaction→function translation map, exploratory registered-scale process–function census, Figures 1–5 and main tables.
