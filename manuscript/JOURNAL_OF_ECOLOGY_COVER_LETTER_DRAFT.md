# Cover letter draft — Journal of Ecology

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit the Research Article **“Habitat fragmentation across plant reproductive life cycles: scale-stable deterioration and recurrent interaction–function proxy failure”** for consideration in *Journal of Ecology*.

Habitat-fragmentation syntheses usually ask whether endpoint classes decline on average. Our manuscript asks a matched plant-ecology question: when interaction and reproductive-function responses are observed under the same landscape exposure, do they move in the same direction, and do focal plant species translate the same perturbation into the same functional outcome?

We assembled five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters comprising 17 historical Hedges-g marginal effects, with explicit within-system dependence and effect-unit rules. On that registered estimand, the pooled five-cluster synthesis rejects complete equality of standardized response magnitudes (`p=0.0121`) and omission of *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.1819`. A mandatory post hoc lnRR audit shows that this robustness classification is scale-dependent: omission of *Serapias* remains significant under raw-unit multivariate delta covariance (`p=8.31×10^-5`) and zero covariance (`p=0.00434`). In *Serapias* itself, C–F is resolved on g but unresolved on lnRR (`p=0.6364`). We therefore retain the g analysis for reproducibility but do not present its separation/robustness pattern as a scale-general ecological conclusion.

The scale audit changed the manuscript's centre of gravity. Across the primary direct stream, all 17 oriented effects are negative on both g and lnRR, but relative response magnitude is scale-sensitive. The more general ecological result is a contrast between **directional coherence and translational non-identifiability**. The three strongest quantitative anchors—*Eucalyptus wandoo*, *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* and Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens*—show representation-stable downstream proxy failure across three continents, three plant families and contrasting pollination/mating systems.

We then froze a broader response-translation audit before using it for inference: eight quantitatively admitted matched I–F programmes plus eight non-overlapping, source-explicit programmes that had been blocked from quantitative synthesis. Across this 16-programme evidence map, the same observed interaction state corresponds to multiple reproductive-function states, and that many-to-many structure survives deletion of every single programme. Put more simply, **interaction decline is neither necessary nor sufficient for reproductive decline** in this frozen mixed-tier evidence universe. The result supports two monitoring error modes—false reassurance and apparent over-warning—without treating qualitative blocked studies as quantitative replications or estimating their prevalence.

Quantity–quality distinctions and imperfect visitation proxies are established concepts in pollination ecology. Our added contribution is therefore narrower: a fragmentation-specific, matched-system demonstration that broad directional deterioration can coexist with non-identifying interaction→function translation. The manuscript keeps evidence tiers separate and treats the 16-programme map as a structural existence result, not a pooled meta-analysis or frequency estimate.

The manuscript is centred on plant ecology and plant–pollinator/reproductive processes, while integrating mating and genetic responses within the same biological systems. We therefore believe it fits *Journal of Ecology*’s interest in research that advances general understanding of plant populations and their interactions with other organisms.

The reviewer-facing manuscript has been prepared for double-anonymous review. Analysis-ready evidence tables and minimal reproduction code are assembled into an anonymised review package that reproduces the five-cluster synthesis, leave-one-out diagnostics, authoritative g-versus-lnRR scale audit, complete I–F sign-geometry census, frozen 16-programme interaction→function translation map, exploratory registered-scale process–function census, Figures 1–5 and main tables.
