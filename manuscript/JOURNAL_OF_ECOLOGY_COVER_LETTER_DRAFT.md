# Cover letter draft — Journal of Ecology

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit the Research Article **“Habitat fragmentation across plant reproductive life cycles: species-specific interaction–function translation and scale-sensitive amplitudes”** for consideration in *Journal of Ecology*.

Habitat-fragmentation syntheses usually ask whether individual endpoints decline on average. Our manuscript asks a different plant-ecology question: when interaction, movement, reproductive and genetic responses are measured within the same fragmented systems, do they deteriorate together or become decoupled because ecological processes operate on different spatial and temporal scales?

We assembled five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters comprising 17 historical Hedges-g marginal effects, with explicit within-system dependence and effect-unit rules. On that registered estimand, the pooled five-cluster synthesis rejects complete equality of standardized response magnitudes (`p=0.0121`) and omission of *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.1819`. A mandatory post hoc lnRR audit shows that this robustness classification is scale-dependent: omission of *Serapias* remains significant under raw-unit multivariate delta covariance (`p=8.31×10^-5`) and zero covariance (`p=0.00434`). In *Serapias* itself, C–F is resolved on g but unresolved on lnRR (`p=0.6364`). We therefore retain the g analysis for reproducibility but do not present its separation/robustness pattern as a scale-general ecological conclusion.

We believe the current contribution is deliberately narrower than a response-bottleneck law. Across the primary direct stream, the scale-stable feature is directional: all 17 oriented effects are negative on both g and lnRR. Cross-scale exploratory auditing retains both downstream function-dominant and upstream process-dominant geometries, but the systems supplying the upstream evidence change with effect scale. The main ecological extension is therefore hypothesis-generating: fragmentation effects may be filtered, buffered, amplified or delayed across mating, reproduction and genetic cohorts. The separate *Eucalyptus wandoo* gradient supplies the clearest genuine sign discordance between interaction and reproductive function.

The manuscript is centred on plant ecology and plant–pollinator/reproductive processes, while integrating mating and genetic responses within the same biological systems. We therefore believe it fits *Journal of Ecology*’s interest in research that advances general understanding of plant populations and their interactions with other organisms.

The reviewer-facing manuscript has been prepared for double-anonymous review. Analysis-ready evidence tables and minimal reproduction code are assembled into an anonymised review package that reproduces the five-cluster synthesis, leave-one-out diagnostics, authoritative g-versus-lnRR scale audit, exploratory registered-scale census, main figures and main tables.
