# Scale-stable interaction-quantity proxy failure — 2026-09-29

## Ecological question

When habitat fragmentation leaves pollinator activity, visitation occurrence or pollen quantity comparatively intact, does that reliably imply that plant reproductive function is also intact?

## Result

No.

Across the current audited process–function catalogue, the **repeated representation-stable resolved mismatch is downstream function loss despite a comparatively better interaction-quantity response**.

Three independent programmes show this topology:

1. **Eucalyptus wandoo — Western Australia**
   - foundation tree; Myrtaceae;
   - bird- and insect-pollinated; mixed mating;
   - pollen tubes increase along the fragmentation gradient while seed production declines;
   - covariance-aware I–F p = 0.0008565;
   - the source itself discusses self-pollen / reduced effective pollen quality despite substantial pollen arrival.

2. **Cardiopetalum calophyllum — Brazilian cerrado**
   - treelet; Annonaceae;
   - allogamous, strongly dichogamous, specialist nitidulid-beetle pollination;
   - pollinator abundance changes weakly while fruit set declines strongly toward smaller fragments;
   - primary I–F p = 0.0031599;
   - alternate follicle/seed endpoints and the isolation sensitivity retain F more negative than I.

3. **Acanthopale pubescens — Kakamega, western Kenya**
   - shrub; Acanthaceae;
   - honey bees treated by the source as the probable effective pollinator;
   - visitation occurrence is maintained/slightly elevated in fragment sites while fruit set declines;
   - registered Hedges-g programme p = 0.006535;
   - lnRR re-expression programme p = 0.001384;
   - the mismatch therefore survives the direct-study effect-scale audit.

These systems span three continents, three plant families, distinct growth forms and very different pollination/mating contexts. Their commonality is **response topology**, not a shared demonstrated mechanism.

## Why this is surprising

Classic fragmentation meta-analysis found a strong positive association between pollination and reproductive-success effect sizes and interpreted reproductive impairment largely through pollination limitation.

The present cross-system exceptions are not random in direction. Where a process–function mismatch remains clearly resolved under the audited representations, the stable cases show reproductive function doing **worse** than the measured interaction quantity.

This does not contradict the global negative association. It identifies a biologically important failure mode hidden by the average relationship:

> **interaction quantity can provide false reassurance about reproductive function under fragmentation.**

## Measurement blind spot

The complete registered I–F set contains **8 independent programmes**.

- quantity-level I endpoints: **8/8**;
- direct effective-mating-quality I endpoints on the same I–F frame: **0/8**.

The corpus repeatedly measures visits, pollinator abundance/occurrence or total pollen/pollen tubes, but not the intermediate variables most capable of explaining why quantity and function diverge:

- compatible conspecific pollen;
- self versus outcross pollen;
- realised paternity / effective donor diversity;
- pollen quality / relatedness;
- post-pollination filtering.

Thus the evidence gap is aligned with the observed failure mode: the missing measurement sits exactly between the quantity proxy and reproductive output.

## What is genuinely general here

The current data do **not** establish one universal bottleneck or one causal mechanism.

The more defensible generalization is:

> **Fragmentation studies should not treat interaction quantity as a sufficient proxy for reproductive function. Cross-system evidence shows that apparently intact or elevated interaction quantity can coexist with reduced reproductive output across taxonomically, geographically and reproductively distinct plant systems.**

## Relation to prior work

- Aguilar et al. (2006) established overall negative fragmentation effects on pollination and reproduction and a positive cross-species association between those effects.
- Aizen & Harder (2007) formalised the distinction between pollen quantity and pollen quality limitation.
- Brudvig et al. (2015) showed experimentally that fragmentation effects on plant–animal interactions and reproduction can be weakly coupled or nuanced.

The novelty here is therefore **not** that pollen quality matters or that interaction and reproduction can ever differ. The new lead is the repeated, audited fragmentation-specific topology across independent natural programmes, combined with the fact that the effective-mating layer is absent from all eight registered I–F measurement frames.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- the three named programmes provide cross-context examples of interaction-quantity proxy failure;
- Kakamega Acanthopale is directly scale-robust under g and lnRR;
- Wandoo and Cardiopetalum provide independent gradient/generalisation support with distinct pollination biology;
- 8/8 I–F programmes measure quantity and 0/8 directly measure effective mating quality;
- the next prospective design should measure quantity → effective mating quality → reproductive function on the same independent sites.

Not allowed:

- all fragmented plants show hidden reproductive failure;
- the three programmes share one causal mechanism;
- downstream proxy failure has an estimable global prevalence from this corpus;
- the 3/3 pattern is a confirmatory sign test;
- pollinator monitoring is useless; the claim is insufficiency as a standalone proxy, not irrelevance.
