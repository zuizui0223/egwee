# Response–effect framework position for the I→F translation result — 2026-10-03

## Purpose

Position the frozen interaction–function translation result against the broader response–effect literature so the manuscript does not claim novelty for an established ecological principle.

## What is already established

### Response and effect are distinct ecological roles

The response–effect framework distinguishes attributes governing how organisms or communities respond to environmental change from attributes governing ecosystem functioning. Lavorel & Garnier (2002) formalized this distinction for plant functional ecology, and later work has repeatedly emphasized that response and effect dimensions may overlap, correlate or decouple.

Ross et al. (2023) similarly review response diversity and note that proxies for environmental response need not directly capture the functional consequences of those responses.

Therefore EGWEE must not claim that it newly discovers the general principle that an ecological response variable can be an imperfect proxy for function.

### The response–effect framework already reaches pollination services

Roquer-Beni et al. (2021) explicitly applied a response–effect trait approach to pollinator communities and apple pollination services, linking environmental/management drivers to pollinator functional responses and fruit set.

Therefore EGWEE must not claim that response–effect decoupling has never been considered in pollination ecology.

## What EGWEE adds

The current synthesis contributes a narrower empirical bridge:

> **Within the same fragmented plant programmes, the qualitative response of measured interaction quantity does not uniquely identify the state of reproductive function.**

This differs from the classic trait-based response–effect framework in four ways:

1. the response axis is an observed ecological process (visitation, pollinator abundance, pollen quantity), not a functional response trait;
2. the effect axis is realized plant reproductive function measured in the same biological programme;
3. the environmental driver is habitat fragmentation / landscape configuration under a matched exposure frame;
4. the evidence map is source- and programme-explicit, preserving quantitative versus qualitative evidence tiers.

Thus the contribution is not a new response–effect theory. It is a fragmentation-specific **process→function translation audit**.

## Empirical result

The frozen 16-programme map contains eight quantitatively admitted matched I–F programmes and eight source-explicit, quantitatively blocked programmes.

The same upstream interaction state maps to different downstream functional states:

- interaction lower → F lower / higher point estimate / no detected loss;
- no detected interaction loss → F lower / no detected loss;
- interaction higher → F lower / similar.

This means interaction quantity alone does not uniquely identify reproductive-function state in the audited fragmentation contexts.

## Relation to existing fragmentation work

Aguilar et al. (2006, 2025) show average negative coupling between pollination and reproductive fitness across species.

Lázaro et al. (2020) show in a four-species fragmentation study that landscape effects on seed production need not be linked directly to visitation rate.

EGWEE therefore does not claim the first example of pollination–function decoupling. Its distinct contribution is the **denominator-frozen cross-programme translation map plus scale-audited quantitative failure anchors**.

## Preferred terminology

Use:

- `interaction–function translation`;
- `non-identifying sentinel`;
- `non-monotonic process→function mapping`;
- `false-reassurance failure mode` for the three stable downstream quantitative anchors.

Avoid presenting these as newly invented general theories.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- the 16-programme frozen evidence map shows existence of multiple I→F translations;
- interaction quantity alone is insufficient to identify reproductive-function state across the audited programmes;
- three independent quantitative programmes provide stable downstream false-reassurance examples;
- this empirical result is naturally interpreted within the established response–effect framework.

Not allowed:

- EGWEE invents the response–effect distinction;
- response–effect decoupling is new to pollination ecology;
- the translation-map categories estimate global frequencies;
- all fragmentation contexts show non-monotonic translation;
- one response–effect mechanism explains the 16 programmes.
