# Exploratory mechanism audit for opposite-sign I–F topology — 2026-09-29

## Status

**Post hoc / hypothesis-generating.**

This note asks what can and cannot explain the opposite-sign interaction–function patterns identified in the scale-independent topology audit.

## Observation

Opposite-sign point geometry occurs in both directions:

- `I+ / F-`: Kakamega *Acanthopale*; *Eucalyptus wandoo*;
- `I- / F+`: Sevenello LARO and POAR; Zurich *Onobrychis viciifolia*; common milkweed.

Only the `I+ / F-` direction is currently resolved at programme level (Kakamega and Wandoo). Reverse-polarity cases remain unresolved.

## Reproductive assurance alone is insufficient

The reverse `I- / F+` point pattern cannot be reduced to autonomous selfing.

- Common milkweed (*Asclepias syriaca*) is highly self-incompatible and relies on insect-mediated pollinia transfer. Its source study nevertheless observed lower pollinator abundance toward the urban centre while discussing relatively high urban reproductive success and proposing increased outcross pollen delivery / pollinator richness as a possible explanation.
- Sevenello LARO (*Lawrencella rosea*) and POAR (*Podolepis aristata*) are strongly insect-pollinator dependent in the source experiment: bagging flowers reduced seed production by roughly 76% and 82%, respectively. Yet their registered edge–core point estimates have lower bee abundance at edges with higher open seed production.

Therefore a positive or buffered reproductive response despite fewer observed interactions can arise even when plants remain strongly dependent on animal pollination.

## Candidate mechanisms

At least four mechanisms can break the abundance→function mapping:

1. **interaction quality / compatible pollen:** fewer visits can carry more outcross or more compatible pollen;
2. **visitor composition:** changes in pollinator identity or behaviour can alter per-visit effectiveness even when total abundance declines;
3. **direct landscape/resource effects on F:** microclimate, resources, herbivory or other landscape components can affect fruit/seed production independently of interaction quantity;
4. **post-pollination filtering:** pollen competition, self-pollen rejection, ovule abortion and resource allocation can decouple pollen arrival from viable seed production.

These mechanisms can act in either direction.

## Source-supported examples

### Eucalyptus wandoo

The source explicitly proposes a quality mechanism: small populations can receive high pollen-tube loads while a higher proportional contribution of self-pollen lowers effective pollen quality and seed set.

### Common milkweed

The species is highly self-incompatible. The urbanization study proposed that higher pollinator richness and more outcrossed pollen could alleviate pollen limitation despite lower total pollinator abundance.

### Sevenello annual forbs

The source reports strong pollinator dependence but also direct and indirect effects of landscape context, local vegetation and bee abundance on seed production. Species-specific landscape paths can therefore offset the simple abundance effect.

### Kakamega Acanthopale

Visitation occurrence is maintained while fruit set declines, but the recovered data do not directly measure compatible pollen or post-pollination filtering. Mechanism remains unresolved.

## General ecological hypothesis

> **Fragmentation changes not only interaction quantity but the conversion efficiency from interactions to successful reproduction. This conversion can rise or fall depending on visitor identity, compatible mating and direct landscape effects.**

This hypothesis is broader than 'pollinator decline causes reproductive decline' and narrower than claiming a universal quantity–quality mechanism.

## Fresh test

The prospective Q→E→F programme should therefore measure:

- Q: interaction abundance / visitation / pollen quantity;
- E: compatible pollen, realised paternity or another effective-mating metric;
- F: reproductive output;
- pollinator composition / identity;
- relevant direct resource or microenvironment covariates.

The key prediction is not necessarily monotonic attenuation. It is that variation in E and direct F covariates should explain cases where Q and F have opposite signs.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- reproductive assurance alone cannot explain all observed reverse-polarity point patterns;
- interaction quality/composition and direct landscape effects are plausible general mechanisms;
- source biology independently supports these mechanisms in at least Wandoo and common milkweed;
- fresh validation should measure effective mating and visitor composition, not interaction quantity alone.

Not allowed:

- claiming any one mechanism explains all programmes;
- treating unresolved reverse-polarity panels as confirmed compensation;
- fitting a post hoc cross-programme trait model from the current small denominator.
