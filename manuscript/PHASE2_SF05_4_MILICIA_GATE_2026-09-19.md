# Phase-2 SF05-4 Milicia primary quantitative gate — 2026-09-19

## Decision

Bizoux et al. 2009 (*Milicia excelsa*) is **closed for the current prespecified Phase-2 pair families**.

This is an exposure/common-frame decision, not an outcome-based exclusion.

## Source geometry

The source samples four Cameroonian populations:

- Mindourou — continuous forest;
- Djoum — continuous forest;
- Biyeyem — agricultural fields/fallows in a more open habitat;
- Belabo — the most open setting, including forest-savanna mosaic and slash-and-burn context.

The paper explicitly treats the populations as differing in demographic characteristics, landscape features, tree density and habitat openness. It does not define the latter two populations as a single replicated fragmentation treatment.

Adult expected heterozygosity and SGS parameters are available on all four populations.

Indirect gene-dispersal distance is estimable only for Mindourou and Djoum, because the open-habitat populations do not show the SGS/IBD pattern required by the estimator.

## Why it does not increment a registered pair

- no F layer is measured;
- no G_offspring cohort is measured;
- the C-like indirect dispersal estimate is absent for the open-habitat populations, so C and G_adult do not share a quantitative four-population effect frame;
- the two open habitats are not one coherent source-defined fragmentation treatment.

Therefore the study cannot increment C-F, G_adult-G_offspring or G_adult-mean(I,F), and no new pair family is created after source inspection.

## Retained use

The study remains useful as mechanistic evidence on how habitat openness, density, SGS and long-distance gene dispersal interact in tropical trees, and for layer-specific G_adult/moderator coverage.
