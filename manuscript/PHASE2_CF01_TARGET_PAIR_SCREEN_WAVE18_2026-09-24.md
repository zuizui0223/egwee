## CFTQ0171 — Attalea humilis linked programme

The current *Journal of Tropical Ecology* paper is primarily a female-reproduction/phenology paper,
but it belongs to the same fragment programme as the companion *Biotropica* paper that measured
reproductive output, seed dispersal and seed predation.

The linked programme contains five source fragments:

- small: AJ-19, VE-26, SH-57;
- large: UN-2400, PA-3500.

Both the reproductive-output sampling and threaded-fruit dispersal experiment omit VE-26. Therefore
the only admissible direct C-F common frame is fixed as:

- small: AJ-19 + SH-57;
- large: UN-2400 + PA-3500.

The source directions are ecologically striking:

- local fruit production is higher in small fragments;
- seed-dispersal quantity is lower in small fragments.

This is a **reproduction–movement sign reversal**.

The public reporting surface, however, does not expose complete four-fragment numeric vectors for
both C and F; large-fragment values are reported partly as group summaries/models/figures. EGWEE
does not digitize figures or attach lower-level palm/station variance to n=2 fragments.

Decision: `blocked_linked_four_fragment_CF_values_not_recoverable`.

Direct C-F programme increment: **0**.

The programme remains a reproduction–movement trade-off / compensation anchor.

## CFTQ0174 — Hedysarum scoparium

This is a strong six-patch pollination study:

- direct bee visitation per patch;
- fruit-set and seed-production pollen-limitation responses;
- patch size and visitation-frequency analyses.

But the source defines patch size as the **number of flowering H. scoparium plants**, not habitat
area, landscape isolation or a fragmented/reference habitat condition.

EGWEE does not turn conspecific population size into habitat fragmentation after screening.

Decision: no quantitative fragmentation I-F admission.

The source is retained as process evidence that visitation and reproductive limitation can decouple:
bee visitation varies strongly among patches and predicts fruit-set pollen limitation more than seed
production limitation.

## CFTQ0180 — Brunsvigia litoralis

The source provides compelling fragmentation mechanism evidence:

- urban fragments lack the effective long-billed Malachite Sunbird pollinator;
- urban flower visitors are primarily nectar-robbing shorter-billed sunbirds;
- urban seed set is low and strongly pollen limited;
- the rural site with the effective pollinator has much higher seed set.

However, the reference condition is one rural site/population. Plants within that site cannot
manufacture independent habitat replication.

Decision: `close_single_reference_site_pseudoreplication`.

The study is retained as a specialized-pollinator-loss / urban-fragmentation mechanism anchor, not a
direct I-F effect.
