# Quantity–function decoupling mechanism audit — 2026-09-27

## Purpose

The current complete I–F census shows a descriptive directional asymmetry: every resolved
interaction–function mismatch has reproductive function more negative than measured interaction or
pollen quantity. This note asks a narrower ecological question:

> Do the three resolved programmes support the same biological mechanism, or only the same response
> geometry?

The answer is **the response geometry is repeated, but mechanism support differs among systems**.

This audit does not change any effect estimate, programme admission, significance decision or
Phase-2 stopping rule.

## 1. *Eucalyptus wandoo* — direct source support for a pollen-quality bottleneck

Primary source:

Llorens, T.M., Yates, C.J., Byrne, M., Elliott, C.P., Sampson, J., Fairman, R., Macdonald, B. &
Coates, D.J. (2018). *Altered Soil Properties Inhibit Fruit Set but Increase Progeny Performance for
a Foundation Tree in a Highly Fragmented Landscape*. Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 6:39.
doi:10.3389/fevo.2018.00039.

Source-supported biology:

- *E. wandoo* is bird- and insect-pollinated and has a mixed mating system.
- Small populations can receive substantial immigrant pollen.
- In the recovered common-population gradient, pollen-tube quantity increases with fragmentation
  severity while seed production declines.
- The source explicitly argues that small populations may receive a larger proportional load of
  self-pollen, lowering effective pollen quality.
- The authors note that incomplete pre- or post-zygotic elimination of selfed ovules can reduce seed
  set even when pollen arrives, and that high pollen immigration need not prevent this reproductive
  cost.

Mechanism status: **source-supported pollen-quality / self-pollen bottleneck**.

EGWEE may therefore use Wandoo as the strongest natural example showing why pollen quantity is not
equivalent to effective reproductive input.

## 2. *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* — source-supported mating-connectivity vulnerability

Primary source:

Elias, M.A.S., Franceschinelli, E.V., Juen, L., Borges, F.J.A., Ferreira, G.M. & Carvalho, F.M.V.
(2012). *Reproductive success of Cardiopetalum calophyllum (Annonaceae) treelets in fragments of
Brazilian savanna*. Journal of Tropical Ecology 28:317–320.
doi:10.1017/S0266467412000120.

Source-supported biology:

- *C. calophyllum* is allogamous.
- Strong dichogamy promotes cross-breeding, making reproduction pollinator-dependent.
- The source identifies the nitidulid beetle *Lobiopa insularis* as the pollinator.
- Fragment size strongly predicts fruit/follicle/seed output, whereas pollinator abundance is not
  detectably related to fragment size.
- The authors propose that smaller and more isolated fragments restrict pollen exchange among
  fragments and increase mating among more closely related plants.
- They additionally note that *L. insularis* is unlikely to supply extensive between-fragment pollen
  movement.

Mechanism status: **source-supported vulnerability of effective pollen exchange / compatible mating,
but not a directly measured causal mediation test**.

Thus Cardiopetalum supports the distinction between local pollinator abundance and landscape-scale
effective pollination.

## 3. Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens* — response mismatch established, mechanism unresolved

Primary source:

Bergsdorf, T. (2006). *Forest fragmentation and plant-pollinator interactions in Western Kenya*.
PhD dissertation, Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität Bonn.

Source-supported biology retained by the recovery:

- honey bees are treated by the source as the most probable effective pollinator for
  *A. pubescens*;
- standardized pollinator occurrence is maintained/slightly higher at fragment sites;
- natural fruit set is sharply lower at those same fragment sites;
- the covariance-aware I–F difference remains resolved after the four-panel programme Bonferroni
  gate.

The available recovered panel does not directly measure compatible pollen, self-pollen, pollen
quality, resource limitation or post-pollination abortion.

Mechanism status: **unresolved**.

Candidate explanations such as pollen quality, mating structure, heterospecific pollen or resource
limitation remain hypotheses and must not be written as the demonstrated cause of the Kakamega
response.

## Cross-system synthesis

The three systems therefore should not be collapsed into one causal mechanism.

What repeats is the **ecological position of the bottleneck**:

```
measured interaction / pollen quantity
             |
             |  can remain relatively intact
             v
effective mating / pollen quality / post-pollination processes
             |
             v
reproductive function
```

Evidence strength differs:

1. **Wandoo:** source explicitly supports lower pollen quality / self-pollen as the explanation.
2. **Cardiopetalum:** reproductive biology and source interpretation support restricted effective
   pollen exchange as a plausible mechanism.
3. **Acanthopale:** only the interaction–function mismatch is established; causal mechanism is open.

This hierarchy is stronger than assigning all three examples to a generic “pollinator decline”
mechanism. It also explains why counting visits, pollinators or pollen tubes can fail to diagnose
reproductive impairment.

## Prospective ecological hypotheses

The next moderator programme should prioritise variables that distinguish **interaction quantity**
from **effective mating quality**:

- self-incompatibility / degree of autonomous reproductive assurance;
- mating system and outcrossing dependence;
- pollinator specialisation;
- source-supported pollen quality or compatible-mate measures;
- capacity for between-fragment pollen movement;
- fragmentation age relative to plant generation time.

These should be frozen before testing whether they predict the coupled versus F-dominant response
regimes. The current three resolved programmes are insufficient for a fitted moderator law.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

> The repeated F-dominant I–F geometry is consistent with a downstream reproductive bottleneck, and
> two of the three resolved systems provide source-level biological support for effective
> pollen-quality/mating-connectivity limitations.

Not allowed:

- all three systems share the same mechanism;
- fragmentation generally reduces pollen quality;
- pollinator abundance causally mediates fruit set in the recovered cross-system corpus;
- the 3/3 directional census proves a universal rule.
