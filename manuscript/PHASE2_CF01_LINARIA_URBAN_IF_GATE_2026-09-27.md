# CFTQ0322 Linaria vulgaris urban I-F gate — 2026-09-27

## Programme

Programme identity: `P2_CF01_LINARIA_URBAN_2019`.

Primary source: Longley (2019), University of Guelph MSc thesis,
*The effect of urbanization on reproduction and selection on floral traits in the wildflower,
Linaria vulgaris*.

## Urbanization classification

The thesis samples along urban-to-rural transects in Guelph, Mississauga and Toronto.

Urban populations are defined from the surrounding built environment and checked against a NASA
impervious-surface map; the thesis describes urban areas as exceeding about 20% impervious surface
in the local 1-km² classification.

This is a legitimate urbanization/generalisation exposure, but it does not automatically make all
thesis experiments one multilayer cluster.

## Q1 — compatible mate availability

Q1 asks whether compatible mate availability affects fruit production in urban populations.

Seeds were collected across three urban-rural transects, but the experiment itself is a common
garden hand-pollination design on maternal plants from **urban populations**.

The three treatments are:

- Urban Home — pollen from the maternal plant's own urban population;
- Urban Away — pollen from another urban population in the same transect;
- Rural Away — pollen from a rural population in the same transect.

The outcome is successful fruit formation after hand crossing.

This is a donor-source / compatible-mate experiment. It is not a natural urbanization-response I
vector measured across the urban-rural population gradient.

## Q2 — urban-rural female fitness and floral selection

Q2 uses a different frame: **seven field populations**, three urban and four rural, along one
40-km transect.

It measures female fitness and floral traits and tests urban-rural differences in fitness/selection.

This is not the same campaign/population frame as the Q1 hand-pollination experiment.

## No common I-F effect unit

The thesis therefore does not supply one:

`population × urbanization exposure × campaign`

frame containing both a natural I interaction response and F.

Joining Q1 compatible-mate treatment to Q2 field fitness would concatenate distinct experiments and
change the estimand.

## Terminal status

`close_no_common_same_exposure_urbanization_IF_frame_across_thesis_questions`

I-F programme increment: **0**.

The thesis remains useful evidence that compatible mate availability can limit fruit formation in
urban-derived plants and that urban/rural populations differ in some reproductive/floral traits.

## No rescue

Do not:

- treat Urban Home/Away/Rural Away pollen-source treatments as urbanization levels;
- join Q1 greenhouse plants to Q2 field populations;
- infer a natural pollinator-visitation I endpoint that was not measured on the Q2 frame;
- count individual flowers/plants as urban landscape replicates;
- use the thesis to validate a finite EGWE/NEE operator.
