# ML020 claim gate

## Admitted empirical object

ML020 is one independent direct Hedges-g programme cluster from the Aizen–Feinsinger 1994 Chaco fragmentation study. Three fully replicated species are dependent subsystems inside the programme and do not increase the cross-system denominator.

## Allowed claim

> In a fifth independently structured direct fragmentation programme, pollination and fruit-set effects deteriorated in broadly concordant fashion rather than showing detectable response-layer separation. After admitting this cluster, the overall five-cluster corpus still rejects exchangeability, but that rejection does not survive omission of ML001 Serapias.

## Required robustness wording

- Full five-cluster Fisher: `p=0.01212432`.
- Omit ML001 Serapias: `p=0.18194353`, no rejection.
- Therefore the present cross-system state-separation conclusion remains Serapias-dependent.

## Forbidden claims

- Do not say the fifth cluster independently replicates state separation.
- Do not say the synthesis is leave-one-cluster-out robust.
- Do not exclude ML020 because its cluster p-value is 1.0.
- Do not launch or select a sixth cluster because ML020 weakened the combined evidence.
- Do not describe ML020 as prospectively outcome-blind; the public Appendix I was visible during recovery.
- Do not count the three species as three independent systems.

## Interpretation

ML020 is scientifically useful because it distinguishes two statements that the four-cluster corpus could not cleanly separate:

1. fragmentation can depress multiple biological layers in the same system;
2. fragmentation effects are necessarily different across layers.

ML020 supports the first statement but not the second under the frozen I-versus-F test. It therefore narrows, rather than inflates, the generality of the state-separation claim.
