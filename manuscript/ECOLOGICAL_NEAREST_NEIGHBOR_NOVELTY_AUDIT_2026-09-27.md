# Ecological nearest-neighbour and novelty audit — 2026-09-27

## Purpose

Define what the current manuscript can and cannot claim as an ecological contribution after the response-coupling reframe.

## What is already established

### Fragmentation commonly reduces pollination and plant reproduction

Aguilar et al. (2006) synthesized fragmentation effects on plant pollination and reproduction and found large negative average effects. Compatibility system explained susceptibility, and pollination and reproductive-success effect sizes were positively related.

The updated synthesis by Aguilar et al. (2025) likewise found negative global land-use-change effects on pollination and female and male fitness. For species in which pollination and female fitness were assessed simultaneously, the reported effect sizes were positively correlated (Pearson r = 0.421, P < 0.01).

Therefore EGWEE must not claim that it newly discovers that fragmentation links pollination and reproduction.

### Pollen quantity and pollen quality are already distinct ecological concepts

Classic and subsequent studies have shown that sufficient pollen quantity can coexist with low compatible-mate diversity, inbreeding or poor pollen quality, and that pollen supplementation can reveal quantity versus quality limitation. Byers (1995) is a clear early example.

Therefore EGWEE must not claim that it newly discovers pollen-quality limitation.

## What the present synthesis adds

### 1. The estimand is within-system response geometry

Earlier global syntheses primarily estimate average effects for endpoint classes and ask how those effects covary across species/studies.

EGWEE instead asks whether process-specific fragmentation responses are distinguishable **within the same biological programme**, using:

- the same fragmentation exposure;
- the same independent habitat/population units;
- explicit within-programme covariance/dependence;
- programme-level multiplicity when several dependent panels exist.

The estimand is therefore not simply whether both I and F decline, nor whether their effects are positively correlated across species. It is the within-programme difference between their standardized fragmentation responses.

### 2. Coupling failure is retained alongside coupling

The corpus contains Chaco and Sevenello programmes without resolved I–F mismatch as well as Wandoo, Cardiopetalum and Kakamega with resolved mismatch.

This allows the manuscript to describe **response regimes** rather than selecting only systems that fit decoupling.

### 3. The complete registered I–F census is directionally asymmetric

Among eight registered I–F programmes:

- seven are pair-testable;
- three resolve a mismatch after their registered programme rule;
- all three resolved cases have F more negative than I;
- four are unresolved;
- one is not pair-testable;
- zero resolve I as more negative than F.

This is descriptive and post hoc. It is not a sign test or global prevalence estimate.

### 4. Quantity–function decoupling is connected to a full life-cycle interpretation

The manuscript does not stop at pollinator counts. It places I–F mismatch beside movement/mating connectivity and adult/offspring genetic responses.

The ecological question becomes where along the life cycle fragmentation effects remain coupled and where a downstream bottleneck emerges.

## Novelty claim ceiling

Strong formulation:

> The contribution is a programme-level synthesis of fragmentation-response coupling, showing that the average pollination–reproduction association documented by previous meta-analyses can conceal within-system response mismatches. In the complete audited I–F programme census, every resolved mismatch is function-dominant, while explicit coupled and unresolved counterexamples remain in the denominator.

Do not claim:

- first evidence that fragmentation reduces reproduction;
- first evidence that pollination and reproduction can differ;
- first evidence for pollen-quality limitation;
- universal F-dominant decoupling;
- a global frequency of coupled versus decoupled regimes;
- a causal common mechanism across Wandoo, Cardiopetalum and Acanthopale.

## Closest literature

- Aguilar et al. 2006 — global fragmentation meta-analysis of pollination and reproduction; average negative effects and cross-effect correlation.
- Aguilar et al. 2025 — updated global land-use meta-analysis of pollination and male/female fitness; simultaneous pollination–female-fitness effect sizes remain positively correlated across species.
- Byers 1995 — explicit pollen quantity versus compatibility/quality limitation in small populations.
- Llorens et al. 2018 — source-level Wandoo mechanism showing why high pollen arrival can coexist with lower reproductive output.

## Implication for writing

Introduction: position EGWEE as moving from endpoint-average effects / cross-species correlation to paired within-programme response geometry.

Discussion: acknowledge that pollen-quality mechanisms are established in plant reproductive ecology; claim novelty for cross-system response-regime synthesis and the complete direction census, not for the mechanism itself.
