# Figure and table captions

## Figure 1. Primary direct-effect evidence geometry

Coverage of admitted biological response layers across the five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters. Filled cells indicate response layers contributing primary Hedges-g effects. Cell labels show the number or structure of marginal effects where multiple dependent outcomes occur: ML003 contains two `G_offspring` cohorts, whereas ML020 contains three plant species sharing the same four Chaco landscapes. The independent primary denominator is five programme/study clusters, not 17 marginal effects. Blank cells represent unmeasured or inadmissible layers and are not coded as zero biological responses.

## Figure 2. Leave-one-cluster-out influence defines the claim ceiling

Full five-cluster Fisher p-value and leave-one-primary-cluster-out p-values plotted on a logarithmic p-value axis. The vertical reference denotes `p=0.05`. The full synthesis rejects complete layer exchangeability (`p=0.01212432`), but removal of ML001 *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.18194353` and removes rejection. Omission of ML002, ML003, ML014 or ML020 retains rejection. The influence analysis is therefore part of the primary interpretation: the present pooled evidence for unequal process responses is not Serapias-independent.

## Figure 3. Fragmentation produces coupled and decoupled interaction–function responses

Four data-derived panels retain each programme's registered effect scale rather than pooling incompatible metrics. Panel A shows the Aizen–Feinsinger Chaco programme on the direct Hedges-g scale: pollen-tube interaction support and fruit set are both lower in small fragments across *Atamisquea emarginata*, *Cercidium australe* and *Prosopis nigra*, with no resolved programme-level I–F separation (`p_ML020=1.0`). Panel B shows *Eucalyptus wandoo* on the Fisher-z gradient scale, where pollen-tube quantity increases with fragmentation severity while seed production declines (I–F `p=0.0008565`). Panel C shows *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* on its Fisher-z fragment-area gradient: pollinator abundance changes weakly while fruit set declines strongly (I–F `p=0.00316`). Panel D shows the Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens* direct Hedges-g panel, where pollinator occurrence is maintained/slightly elevated in fragment sites while fruit set is much lower (I–F `p=0.00163`). Open circles denote the interaction/pollen-quantity response and filled squares denote reproductive function. Effect magnitudes are interpreted within panels only; the figure compares ecological response geometry, not numerical effect size across Hedges-g and Fisher-z families.


## Table 1. Admitted primary direct-effect clusters

Independent programme/study clusters contributing to the primary Hedges-g synthesis of within-system fragmentation-response coupling. For each cluster the table reports the biological system, independent fragmentation unit, source-defined direct contrast, admitted primary layers, number of marginal effects, within-cluster dependence treatment, cluster-level p-value and interpretation. ML020 appears once because its three plant species share the same four landscape programmes and are dependent subsystems. The separate ML015 *Eucalyptus wandoo* Fisher-z gradient analysis is not included in the primary table or Fisher statistic.

## Table 2. Complete registered interaction–function programme census

All eight independent programmes in the current registered corpus that contain eligible interaction/pollen-quantity (I) and reproductive-function (F) information. The table reports the source identifier, effect family, number of dependent I–F panels, whether a registered within-programme I–F comparison is possible, the programme-adjusted p-value where applicable, census status and ecological interpretation. Seven programmes are pair-testable: three resolve an I–F mismatch and all three have reproductive function more negative than interaction quantity, while four are unresolved. The Pritchard orchard-isolation programme is retained as not testable because paired covariance cannot be reconstructed. Direct Hedges-g and gradient Fisher-z programmes are not numerically pooled in this table; the census summarizes within-programme response direction only.

## Supplementary Table S1. Registered multilayer cluster recovery flow

Terminal status of all 16 formally registered multilayer cluster attempts. Five clusters satisfy the direct Hedges-g primary admission contract, ML015 is retained only as separate Fisher-z gradient generalisation evidence, eight attempts are closed because the required same-frame exposure/effect/variance/covariance or public source data cannot be reconstructed under the frozen effect-unit rules, and two attempts are closed because one condition is represented by only a single independent landscape/reference population. Closed or inaccessible systems are not coded as zero biological effects. The table documents why the final five-cluster primary denominator is determined by estimand and effect-unit validity rather than statistical significance.

## Supplementary Table S2. Dependence sensitivity and covariance-free certification boundary

Cluster-level Bonferroni p-values and five-cluster Fisher results under three dependence regimes. The canonical row uses the frozen paired-unit covariance reconstructions. The zero-covariance row leaves all marginal effects and variances unchanged but sets every off-diagonal within-cluster covariance to zero; the full Fisher result remains below 0.05 (`p=0.03860161`). The Cauchy–Schwarz row uses the pairwise covariance lower bound `Cov_ij=-sqrt(V_i V_j)`, which maximises each pairwise contrast variance and provides a covariance-free certification bound from marginal information alone; the corresponding Fisher p-value is `0.28061178`. The latter is not a jointly fitted alternative covariance model. Omission of ML001 *Serapias lingua* does not reject in any of the three regimes.

## Supplementary Table S3. All admitted primary marginal effects

All 17 marginal Hedges-g effects entering the five admitted direct clusters. For each effect the table reports the system or dependent subsystem, response layer, endpoint, source-defined fragmentation contrast, independent-unit type, independent sample sizes, effect, sampling variance, standard error and marginal 95% confidence interval. Negative effects indicate lower biological support/function under fragmentation after the declared orientation rule. These 17 rows are not 17 independent meta-analytic replicates: the independent denominator remains five programme/study clusters. The confidence intervals are marginal endpoint intervals and do not replace the covariance-aware within-cluster tests of whether process-specific fragmentation responses differ.

## Supplementary Figure S1. All 17 primary marginal fragmentation effects

Forest display of the 17 effects in Supplementary Table S3. Points are Hedges' g and horizontal lines are marginal 95% confidence intervals; zero denotes no standardized fragmented-versus-reference difference. ML001 *Serapias lingua* is plotted on a separately labelled left horizontal scale because its standardized magnitudes are an order of magnitude larger than the other clusters, partly reflecting very small between-population standardizing SDs. ML002, ML003, ML014 and ML020 share the right scale. The dual scale is a visualisation device only: no values are rescaled or transformed for inference, and marginal confidence intervals are not within-system response-difference tests.
