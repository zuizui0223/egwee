# Figure and table captions

## Figure 1. Primary direct-effect evidence geometry

Coverage of admitted biological response layers across the five independent direct fragmented-versus-reference programme/study clusters. Filled cells indicate response layers contributing primary Hedges-g effects. Cell labels show the number or structure of marginal effects where multiple dependent outcomes occur: ML003 contains two `G_offspring` cohorts, whereas ML020 contains three plant species sharing the same four Chaco landscapes. The independent primary denominator is five programme/study clusters, not 17 marginal effects. Blank cells represent unmeasured or inadmissible layers and are not coded as zero biological responses.

## Figure 2. Historical Hedges-g leave-one-cluster-out influence

Full five-cluster Fisher p-value and leave-one-primary-cluster-out p-values for the **registered Hedges-g primary estimand**, plotted on a logarithmic p-value axis with a reference at `p=0.05`. The full g-scale synthesis rejects equality of standardized within-system responses (`p=0.01212432`), whereas omission of ML001 *Serapias lingua* gives `p=0.18194353`. This is a scale-specific historical result, not the final scale-independent claim ceiling. Figure 4 shows that the omit-*Serapias* robustness classification changes under oriented-lnRR sensitivity and dependence assumptions.

## Figure 3. Interaction–function response geometries on their registered scales

Four descriptive examples retain each programme's registered effect scale rather than pooling incompatible metrics. Panel A shows the Aizen–Feinsinger Chaco programme on the direct Hedges-g scale: pollen-tube interaction support and fruit set are both lower in small fragments across *Atamisquea emarginata*, *Cercidium australe* and *Prosopis nigra*, but the programme-level I–F difference is unresolved (`p_ML020=1.0`). This is **not evidence that the true I and F effects are equal**. Panel B shows *Eucalyptus wandoo* on the Fisher-z gradient scale, where pollen-tube quantity is positive while seed production is negative (I–F `p=0.0008565`), providing genuine sign discordance. Panel C shows *Cardiopetalum calophyllum* on its Fisher-z fragment-area gradient: pollinator abundance changes weakly while fruit set declines strongly (I–F `p=0.00316`); this is a registered-scale magnitude mismatch, not a cross-estimand ordering claim. Panel D shows the Kakamega *Acanthopale pubescens* direct Hedges-g panel, where pollinator occurrence is maintained/slightly elevated in fragment sites while fruit set is much lower (I–F `p=0.00163`). Open circles denote interaction/pollen quantity and filled squares reproductive function. Effect magnitudes are interpreted within panels only. Panels B and D are the clearest qualitative sign-discordance examples; panel A is an unresolved same-direction example.


## Figure 4. Relative response geometry is estimand-scale dependent

Panel A shows the *Serapias lingua* rank reversal between the historical Hedges-g scale and the oriented log-response-ratio sensitivity. Absolute g magnitudes rank adult genetic state > pollen immigration > fruit set, whereas absolute lnRR magnitudes rank pollen immigration > fruit set > adult genetic state; the C–F lnRR contrast is unresolved under the carried endpoint-correlation proxy (p≈0.605). Panel B shows the leave-one-ML001 Fisher p-value under the historical g analysis and three lnRR dependence treatments. The classification crosses the 0.05 threshold across estimand/dependence choices: the g analysis does not reject after removing ML001, two working lnRR treatments do, and the covariance-free maximum-variance lnRR boundary does not certify rejection. Panel C shows the scale-stable qualitative result: all 17 primary direct effects are negative on both oriented g and oriented lnRR. Panel D records the exploratory biological hypothesis generated by lnRR—possible attenuation and cohort lag across mating, performance and genetic states—while explicitly retaining Chaco as a counterexample to universal attenuation. lnRR is a sensitivity scale rather than a replacement primary estimand.

Independent programme/study clusters contributing to the primary Hedges-g synthesis of within-system fragmentation-response coupling. For each cluster the table reports the biological system, independent fragmentation unit, source-defined direct contrast, admitted primary layers, number of marginal effects, within-cluster dependence treatment, cluster-level p-value and interpretation. ## Table 2. Estimand-scale sensitivity of the direct synthesis

Cluster-level and cross-cluster p-values under the historical Hedges-g primary analysis and the oriented lnRR sensitivity. lnRR is shown under three dependence treatments: existing dimensionless endpoint-correlation proxies carried to lnRR marginal variances, zero covariance, and a pairwise Cauchy–Schwarz maximum-contrast-variance boundary. The FULL and OMIT_ML001 rows expose the key inferential instability: leave-one-*Serapias* robustness changes with estimand scale and dependence treatment. These columns are sensitivity analyses, not interchangeable estimates of one common parameter.

## Supplementary Table S1. Registered multilayer cluster recovery flow

Terminal status of all 16 formally registered multilayer cluster attempts. Five clusters satisfy the direct Hedges-g primary admission contract, ML015 is retained only as separate Fisher-z gradient generalisation evidence, eight attempts are closed because the required same-frame exposure/effect/variance/covariance or public source data cannot be reconstructed under the frozen effect-unit rules, and two attempts are closed because one condition is represented by only a single independent landscape/reference population. Closed or inaccessible systems are not coded as zero biological effects. The table documents why the final five-cluster primary denominator is determined by estimand and effect-unit validity rather than statistical significance.

## Supplementary Table S2. Dependence sensitivity and covariance-free certification boundary

Cluster-level Bonferroni p-values and five-cluster Fisher results under three dependence regimes. The canonical row uses the frozen paired-unit covariance reconstructions. The zero-covariance row leaves all marginal effects and variances unchanged but sets every off-diagonal within-cluster covariance to zero; the full Fisher result remains below 0.05 (`p=0.03860161`). The Cauchy–Schwarz row uses the pairwise covariance lower bound `Cov_ij=-sqrt(V_i V_j)`, which maximises each pairwise contrast variance and provides a covariance-free certification bound from marginal information alone; the corresponding Fisher p-value is `0.28061178`. The latter is not a jointly fitted alternative covariance model. Omission of ML001 *Serapias lingua* does not reject in any of the three regimes.

## Supplementary Table S3. All admitted primary marginal effects

All 17 marginal Hedges-g effects entering the five admitted direct clusters. For each effect the table reports the system or dependent subsystem, response layer, endpoint, source-defined fragmentation contrast, independent-unit type, independent sample sizes, effect, sampling variance, standard error and marginal 95% confidence interval. Negative effects indicate lower biological support/function under fragmentation after the declared orientation rule. These 17 rows are not 17 independent meta-analytic replicates: the independent denominator remains five programme/study clusters. The confidence intervals are marginal endpoint intervals and do not replace the covariance-aware within-cluster tests of whether process-specific fragmentation responses differ.

## Supplementary Figure S1. All 17 primary marginal fragmentation effects

Forest display of the 17 effects in Supplementary Table S3. Points are Hedges' g and horizontal lines are marginal 95% confidence intervals; zero denotes no standardized fragmented-versus-reference difference. ML001 *Serapias lingua* is plotted on a separately labelled left horizontal scale because its standardized magnitudes are an order of magnitude larger than the other clusters, partly reflecting very small between-population standardizing SDs.
